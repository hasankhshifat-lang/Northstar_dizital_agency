# YouTube monetization & Community Guidelines compliance (all channels)

Owner rule, 2026-11-24: "Must follow all the rules of YouTube for getting monetization and community guidelines for
all my channels, and check the rules monthly for any updates. Avoid all those which can be reason to become demonetized."

Applies to **every** channel and every video, Short, title, description, tag, thumbnail, playlist, Community post
and comment reply: Which Life Next, As Printed, The Overthinking Pad, Foot All FC (YouTube), and the Scientific Bithi
and Which Life Next Facebook Pages (section 8). Applies in chat and in every scheduled task.

**Priority:** if this rulebook conflicts with any other file or scheduled task prompt (volume targets, slots,
formats, topic lists), the rule that keeps the channel safe and monetizable wins. Missing a publish slot is always
better than publishing something that breaks a policy. Report the conflict to the owner.

Last verified against YouTube's own pages: **2026-11-24** (see the change log, section 11). The monthly scheduled task
"YouTube policy compliance: monthly check" re-checks the sources in section 12 and updates this file.

---

## 1. The biggest risk for our channels: "inauthentic content"

We publish a lot (Which Life Next 3 Shorts a day + long videos, As Printed daily, Foot All FC 3 a day, The
Overthinking Pad 4+4 a week), with AI voices and reusable engines/templates. That is allowed, but it is exactly the
profile YouTube's **inauthentic content** policy looks at (renamed from "repetitious content" on 2025-07-15). YouTube's
channel monetization policy says content must not be "mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative"; content
that "appears to be produced using a template" where "each video doesn't deliver creative, educational, or other
value", and AI content "made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production", can't
earn money. Reviewers judge the **whole channel**, not one video, and the result is usually removal from YPP for the
channel, not a single demonetized video.

Using AI is not the problem: YouTube says disclosing AI "alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its
eligibility to earn money". The problem is sameness and low value. So every video must:

1. Have its **own topic, own research and own script**, written for that video (no swapping names/dates into a
   fixed script, no "part 2" that repeats part 1).
2. Deliver **real educational or entertainment value**: a specific true story, real facts with sources, a clear
   payoff. Fact sheets with sources stay in the repo (FACTS.md / script file).
3. Show **visible variation** across videos: different scene plans, shot lists, settings, characters' actions,
   camera reasons and openings. The engine/look can stay the same (that is branding); the content inside it must not.
4. Never be a **near-duplicate** of an earlier video on any of our channels (same story + same angle + same
   structure). Re-using a topic needs a genuinely new angle and a new script; say so in TOPIC.md.
5. Never be **filler to hit a quota**: if a run can't make a distinct, checked, good video in time, skip the slot
   and report it. The volume targets in CLAUDE.md are maximums, not obligations.
6. Show **human creative direction**: the storyboard, the topic report and the scene inspection are the record of
   editorial decisions. Keep them in the repo for every video.
7. Keep the channel **coherent**: one clear theme per channel, matching the About section; don't mix unrelated
   content into a channel to chase views.

Warning signs to stop and fix before uploading: two videos this week with the same hook line or same first frame
layout; titles that only differ by a name or number; the same B-roll/scene order in consecutive videos; a script
that could be re-used for another topic by replacing nouns.

## 2. Reused content

The reused content policy covers re-uploading material "without adding significant original commentary,
substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value". It is separate from copyright and applies "even
if you have permission". So:
- Archive scans, public-domain photos, maps and documents (As Printed, Which Life Next) are only the raw material:
  every video adds original narration, analysis, story structure and animation. Never post a scan slideshow with
  thin narration.
- No compilations, no clips of other creators' videos, no re-uploads of our own videos across channels, no TV/match
  footage, no "top 10" built from other people's footage.
- A Short cut from our own long video is fine (it is our original work); it must be re-edited for vertical with its
  own hook, not a plain crop.

## 3. Community Guidelines (removal + strikes)

First violation = warning; then a strike; **3 strikes in 90 days = channel termination**. Active strikes also block
YPP eligibility. Never risk a strike. For each policy, what it means for us:

- **Spam, deceptive practices and scams:** no misleading titles/thumbnails/descriptions (the video must deliver what
  the packaging promises); no keyword or tag stuffing; at most 3–5 relevant hashtags (more than 15 makes YouTube
  ignore all of them and looks spammy); no fake engagement (no sub4sub, bought views/likes/comments, view bots,
  incentivised clicks); no comment spam; no links to unsafe sites; no repeated identical comments or replies.
- **Misinformation:** no false claims about elections, health/medicine (against health authorities' guidance), or
  real events. Accuracy rules of `docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md` apply: no invented quotes, evidence or events;
  disputed facts labelled.
- **Medical misinformation (The Overthinking Pad):** education, not medical advice; never contradict WHO/national
  health authorities on treatment; no "cure" claims; flag disputed or non-replicated studies.
- **Suicide, self-harm and eating disorders:** never show methods or details; no glorifying; when the topic touches
  them, a calm, educational framing and a help line in the description (e.g. "If you are struggling, contact your
  local emergency number or a crisis line such as 988 in the US."). Content focused on eating disorders is not
  eligible for full ads (advertiser guidelines, Jan 2026), so avoid it as a main topic.
- **Violent or graphic content:** history and war are allowed in educational/documentary context; no gore, no
  close-ups of wounds, executions or bodies; no real graphic footage. Keep violence implied and stylised.
- **Harassment and cyberbullying (Foot All FC caricatures, real people in history):** jokes target the moment, never
  a person's looks, ethnicity, religion, disability or private life; no insults, no doxxing, no targeting of living
  private individuals. Living public figures: no defamatory claims, no realistic fake depiction.
- **Hate speech:** no content promoting hatred against protected groups. Historical racism (newspapers, quotes,
  laws) may be explained in context, but never read slurs aloud, never show slurs large on screen or in
  thumbnails/titles, and always frame them as history being explained.
- **Harmful or dangerous acts (Scientific Bithi experiments, history stunts):** no dangerous challenges or
  experiments viewers could copy (fire, chemicals, electricity, heights) without a clear "don't try this" and safe
  framing; prefer demonstrations that are safe or obviously animated.
- **Child safety:** no sexualised or endangering depictions of minors; cartoon children only in safe, everyday
  situations.
- **Nudity and sexual content:** none, including historical art in thumbnails.
- **Violent extremism:** no glorification of terrorist groups or their symbols; educational history only, without
  their propaganda material.
- **Impersonation:** never present a channel or a voice as a real person or organisation; never an AI voice or face
  that imitates a real living person.
- **Firearms, drugs, regulated goods:** educational/historical mention only; no instructions, no sales links.

## 4. Advertiser-friendly guidelines (yellow icon = limited or no ads)

A video can stay up but earn little or nothing. The **title, thumbnail and first seconds** weigh most.
- No profanity in titles, thumbnails or the opening; avoid strong profanity anywhere.
- No shocking or graphic imagery in thumbnails or the first seconds (gore, distress, people who "appear to be
  young" in distress: ineligible since Mar 2026).
- Violence/death in educational or documentary context can monetize (clarified Aug 2026) when it's not graphic or
  sensationalised.
- Controversial issues (abuse, self-harm, etc.) only non-graphic and dramatized (Jan 2026); child abuse and eating
  disorders as the focus stay ineligible for full ads.
- Hateful/derogatory content: critiquing ideas may be eligible (Nov 2026), but hate or harassment of people or
  protected groups is not.
- Sensitive events (wars, tragedies, disasters still in the news): avoid exploiting them for views; no clickbait
  around current tragedies.
- When unsure, self-certify honestly in Studio; never hide sensitive content behind a neutral thumbnail.

## 5. Copyright, trademarks and music

- Music: only tracks we have the right to use (YouTube Audio Library, our own generated/licensed music with a
  licence that allows monetized YouTube use). No popular songs, no "fair use" excuses. Note the source in the
  project folder.
- Images/footage: public domain or licensed, source and rights recorded (thumbnail rules already require this).
  Pre-1929 US publications and LoC "no known restrictions" items are the safe default.
- **No match footage, broadcast clips, club crests, league logos or sponsor marks** (Foot All FC); generic kits only.
- Fonts and brand assets: only licensed ones in the repo.
- Never re-upload anything that got a Content ID claim; fix the cause first. Never dispute a claim without a real
  right.

## 6. AI and synthetic media

- "Altered or synthetic content" / "AI use" = **Yes on every upload, every channel** (owner rule 2026-11-17;
  `yt.py upload`/`update` do it). YouTube requires disclosure of realistic AI content and may remove content or
  suspend YPP for creators who "consistently choose not to disclose".
- Descriptions say "Narration voiced with AI." (and AI sound/music if used).
- Never an AI face or realistic voice of a real person; never make a real person appear to say or do something they
  didn't. Caricatures stay obviously cartoon.

## 7. Settings, metadata and channel health

- **Made for kids:** set honestly. Our YouTube channels are made for a general/teen-and-adult audience, so "not
  made for kids" (`selfDeclaredMadeForKids=false`) is right for them; if a video is really aimed at under-13s, it
  must be marked made for kids (that turns off personalised ads and comments). Never mark it wrongly to get
  comments or ads.
- Titles, descriptions, tags and thumbnails describe the video accurately. Tags: relevant only, no competitor
  names, no unrelated trending terms.
- Description: sources, AI disclosure line, no misleading links, no affiliate/sponsor content without the paid
  promotion box ticked in Studio.
- Comment replies (auto-reply tasks): varied, human-sounding, on-topic, never the same text pasted many times,
  never links, never asking for subs/likes in exchange for anything.
- Keep each channel active (YouTube may turn off monetization after **6+ months** without uploads).
- About section and channel name match what the channel actually publishes.
- 2-Step Verification on, one AdSense for YouTube account per the owner, no active strikes.

## 8. Facebook Pages (Scientific Bithi, Which Life Next)

Meta's Community Standards and Partner Monetization / Content Monetization policies are stricter than many think and
follow the same logic: original content (no unoriginal or reused material), no misleading packaging or engagement
bait ("comment YES", "share if you agree"), no limited-originality clips, no dangerous acts for young viewers, music
only with rights. Use the same checklist (section 9) for Facebook posts.

## 9. Pre-publish checklist (run on every video, before upload; record the result)

Write "Compliance: pass" (or the failed items) in the upload's row in `social/youtube-schedule-log.md` /
`social/facebook-page-log.md` (or the channel's Facebook log). Any **No** that can't be fixed = don't upload; report.

1. Original: own topic, own research, own script; not a near-duplicate of any of our videos (check the channel's
   learning file and schedule log for the last 30 days).
2. Real value: specific facts with sources in the repo; clear payoff; no filler made to hit a slot.
3. Visible variation from the last 5 videos on the channel (hook, first frame, structure, scenes).
4. Packaging honest: title and thumbnail match what the video delivers; no shock, gore, sex, profanity; 2–5 word
   headline that adds to the title.
5. No Community Guidelines risk from section 3 (violence, hate, harassment, dangerous acts, misinformation,
   self-harm, child safety, impersonation).
6. Advertiser-friendly: clean title/thumbnail/opening; sensitive topics non-graphic and educational (section 4).
7. Rights: every image, clip, font, sound and music track is ours, public domain or licensed, with the source
   recorded; no crests, logos, match/TV footage.
8. AI disclosure YES set and "Narration voiced with AI." in the description; no realistic fake of a real person.
9. Metadata: accurate description with sources, 3–5 relevant hashtags, relevant tags, made-for-kids set honestly,
   paid promotion declared if any.
10. Health/medical, finance or legal claims: educational, sourced, no advice or guarantees.

## 10. Monthly audit (done by the monthly scheduled task)

1. Re-read every source in section 12; compare with this file; record changes in section 11 with date and source;
   update the rules here (the task may edit this file and push to main; changes to CLAUDE.md, runbooks or other
   scheduled tasks go to the owner as proposals).
2. Audit each channel's last 30 days of uploads (titles, descriptions, tags, thumbnails, AI disclosure, made-for-kids,
   repetition between videos) against section 9, read-only, and list risks with a fix proposal.
3. Check channel status where the API allows (YPP status / strikes are only visible in Studio: write "check in
   Studio" for the owner rather than guessing).
4. Report to the owner: what changed in the rules, risks found per channel, actions needed from the owner (e.g.
   accepting new YPP terms in Studio).
