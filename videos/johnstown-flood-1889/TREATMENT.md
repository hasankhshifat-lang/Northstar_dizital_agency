# TREATMENT — "Fourteen Miles" (The Johnstown Flood, 1889)

3D Map Explainer · YouTube Short / Reel · 1080×1920 · 24 fps · ~48 s · English narration + burned-in subtitles + .srt

## 1. Three candidate structures

- **A. The funnel.** Globe → Pennsylvania → the valley → the dam → back out. Clear, but it spends the first
  seconds on a globe nobody needs to find Pennsylvania, and it ends on the cliché pull-back.
- **B. One line, with a clock.** Start on the town, travel *up* the valley to the dam (the setup), cut the
  dam open (the problem), then let the river ignite back *down* the valley while a clock runs 3:10 → 4:07
  (the turn), and end on the number held over the town. The same 14 miles are crossed twice: once calm, once burning.
- **C. Before / after on one terrain.** The valley with the lake, then without. Honest, but static: the
  event itself (the water moving) never happens on screen.

**Chosen: B.** The story *is* a line (14 miles of river) and a time (under an hour). Travelling the line
twice gives the hook (a quiet town), the turn (the same valley, now lit), and an ending that echoes the opening
shot. Opening: a single glowing point on a dark region (not the globe). Ending: the last quantity held over the
place (not back to the world). Score shape: a calm pluck pulse that climbs, drops to a pedal under the
section, half a bar of silence, then drums and cello spiccato under the run, ending on one held horn note.

## 2. Logline and arc

A town in a narrow valley, a lake 14 miles above it held back by packed earth — and the hour in 1889 when
that earth gave way.

Setup: Johnstown, a quiet valley, a lake upstream. → Turn: the dam fails at 3:10; the river lights up
all the way down. → Ending: 4:07, the Stone Bridge, the fire, the count: 2,209.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Newsroom 3D terrain explainers of river disasters** (flood and dam-failure pieces built on elevation data).
  Learn: travel along the real channel, one fact per screen, measurements drawn on the ground, numbers sourced
  in a corner. Don't take: their colours, label styles, camera paths, typefaces.
- **Documentary aerials at golden hour.** Learn: low raking light makes a narrow valley read as a trench.
  Don't take: any shot or framing.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo 90 bpm (bar = 2.667 s), 4/4. Sections:
- **Intro** (bars 1–2, 0–5.3): low plucked-string pulse on quarter notes, one glock ping on the pin pulse (0.4 s).
- **Climb** (bars 3–5, 5.3–13.3): pulse + harp arpeggio rising; glock ping when the ruler snaps (≈ 8.5 s) and when the lake fills (≈ 10.5 s).
- **Section** (bars 6–8, 13.3–20.5): music thins to a low sine pedal with harmonics + bowed vibraphone; undersea hush.
- **Silence** (20.5–22.4): half a bar of nothing (only thin air).
- **Run** (bars 9–13, 22.4–35): frame drum on every downbeat, cello spiccato eighths, sub thump + shimmer when the head ignites (22.8 s); pitch climbs each bar.
- **Bridge** (bars 13–15, 35–41): pizzicato per pillar slab (one per 5 ft, 8 slabs); horn swell under "40 feet".
- **End** (41–48): everything drops except a held low horn + pedal; one last glock ping as 2,209 lands.
