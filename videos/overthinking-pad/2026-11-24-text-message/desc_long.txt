Your phone buzzes. The reply to your long, careful message says... "ok." With a period. And within a minute your brain has decided they're annoyed. Psychologists have actually tested how well we read tone in typed messages, and the answer says more about the brain doing the reading than about the message.

On this page: the 2005 Kruger & Epley experiments (e-mail vs voice: listeners got about 3 in 4 right, readers were at coin-flip level and just as sure), why friends did no better than strangers, how your brain fills the missing voice with your own, the period study, the "liking gap" and the "reaching out" gap (people like you, and appreciate hearing from you, more than you think), and three evidence-tied things to try next time a short text sends you spiraling.
