1. **Proposed topic.** Is decision fatigue real? The famous Israeli parole-board study, the ego-depletion "willpower
   battery" behind it, how both were checked (case-order critique, Glöckner's simulation, the 23-lab and 36-lab
   replications, the glucose critique), what still shows up in the field (antibiotics, hand hygiene, analysts), and
   what to do with it.
2. **Why viewers care.** Everyone knows the 4 p.m. "I can't decide what's for dinner" feeling, and many have heard the
   hungry-judge fact. The video tells them which part to believe.
3. **Central conflict / mystery.** A famous, shocking graph (65% → 0 → 65%) and a famous theory; then the check. Myth or
   real? The answer is a third option: smaller and different (drift to the default).
4. **Strongest angle.** Myth-check with a real turn: "decision fatigue isn't a myth; it's smaller and stranger."
5. **Opening hook.** "It's four in the afternoon... So... what do you want for dinner? And your brain just... stops."
6. **Three titles.**
   - Is Decision Fatigue Real? What the Hungry Judges Study Got Wrong (chosen)
   - The Hungry Judges Study Everyone Quotes Is Probably Wrong
   - Why You Make Worse Decisions at 4 PM (It's Not Your Willpower)
7. **Expected interest.** High: decision fatigue / willpower is a staple productivity and psychology topic; most
   videos repeat the judges and the battery uncritically, so an honest, sourced myth-check is differentiated.
8. **Classification.** Evergreen.
9. **Visual opportunities.** The saw-tooth parole chart drawn live; the judge's bench and empty plate; cookies vs
   radishes; the willpower battery; a ring of 23 labs; the fork (think it through vs the default) as the recurring
   motif; soap dispenser, prescription pad, analyst screen; if-then card; the 4 p.m. office as the callback.
10. **Reliable sources.** Danziger et al. 2011 (PNAS); Weinshall-Margel & Shapard 2011 (PNAS); Glöckner 2016 (JDM);
    Lakens 2017 (blog, effect-size argument); Baumeister et al. 1998 (JPSP); Vohs et al. 2008 (JPSP); Gailliot et al.
    2007 (JPSP); Kurzban 2010 (Evol Psychol); Hagger et al. 2016 (PPS); Vohs et al. 2021 (Psych Sci); Linder et al.
    2014 (JAMA IM); Dai et al. 2015 (J Appl Psychol); Hirshleifer et al. 2019 (JFE); Inzlicht & Schmeichel 2012 (PPS);
    Job, Dweck & Walton 2010 (Psych Sci); Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (AESP); Lewis 2012 (Vanity Fair). See FACTS.md.
