Notebook doodle (channel engine, Sketchnote base). Per scene: purpose · what is drawn (draw order = the cue words in episode.js) · shot and why · SFX. Characters react (L.react) and props sit at the hand positions person() returns.

## hook1
> It's four in the afternoon. You've answered forty emails, picked a lunch, said yes to a meeting you didn't want... and now someone asks you one more question. So... what do you want for dinner? And your brain just... stops.

- **Purpose:** hook: the 4 p.m. freeze, shown before it is named
- **Shot:** opens close on the tired face at the desk from frame 0, pulls back as the day piles up (emails, lunch, meeting), holds wide when the dinner question lands; the spinner over the head = the brain stops
- **SFX:** office hum; a mail ping per email; a soft "pop" on the question; silence on "stops"

## hook2
> There's a name for that feeling. Decision fatigue. And it's backed by one of the most famous studies in psychology: judges who granted parole... depending on how long it had been since their last meal.

- **Purpose:** name it + the famous study: judges, parole, meals
- **Shot:** pan right; the label first, then the bench and the plate; push on the clock between the plate and the gavel
- **SFX:** a gavel tap on "parole"; a plate clink on "meal"

## hook3
> But here's the part almost nobody mentions. That study got checked. So did the theory behind it. And some of it... didn't survive.

- **Purpose:** the twist: the study and the theory got checked; some did not survive
- **Shot:** pan right; the study card is stamped CHECKED, a magnifier sweeps it; push on the torn half
- **SFX:** stamp thud on "checked"; paper tear on "survive"

## hook4
> So is decision fatigue real? The honest answer is stranger than yes or no. By the end, you'll know which parts to believe... and what actually helps.

- **Purpose:** the promise: real or myth? stranger than yes/no; which parts to believe + what helps

## c1a
> Start with the study that made decision fatigue famous. In twenty eleven, three researchers, Shai Danziger, Jonathan Levav and Liora Avnaim-Pesso, looked at parole hearings in Israel.

- **Purpose:** who and where: Danziger, Levav, Avnaim-Pesso, 2011, Israeli parole hearings
- **Shot:** pan right from the banner; the three researcher heads are written in, then the place tag; slow push
- **SFX:** pen; page tap on "Israel"

## c1b
> They collected more than a thousand rulings, made by eight experienced judges over ten months. Each working day was split into three sessions by two food breaks: a morning snack, and lunch.

- **Purpose:** the data: 1,000+ rulings, 8 judges, 10 months; the day = 3 sessions split by 2 food breaks
- **Shot:** pan right; the stack of rulings grows on the left, then the day bar is drawn left to right with the two meals dropped in
- **SFX:** paper flicks for the stack; a cup clink and a plate clink

## c1c
> Then they plotted one simple thing: the chance that a prisoner was granted parole, depending on where their case fell in the session.

- **Purpose:** set up the one measure: chance of parole by position in the session
- **Shot:** pan right; empty axes are drawn and labelled (the viewer reads the question before the data)
- **SFX:** ruler-straight pen strokes

## c1d
> At the start of a session, about sixty five percent of requests were granted. Then, case by case, the line slid down... until, just before the break, it was close to zero.

- **Purpose:** session 1: 65% at the start, sliding to near zero before the break
- **Shot:** the camera stays on the chart and follows the pen down the slope (the drop is the story)
- **SFX:** a descending pluck under the slope

## c1e

- **Purpose:** the break: they eat, the rate jumps back to 65% and slides again
- **Shot:** slow pan right along the chart to the second session; the plate is dropped in at the gap; the line jumps
- **SFX:** plate clink on "ate"; an upward pluck on the jump

## c1x
> Three sessions, three slides, three jumps. On the graph it looks like the edge of a saw. And it is very hard to look at that saw and not feel a little chill.

- **Purpose:** the full saw: three sessions, three slides, three jumps (the picture people remember)
- **Shot:** pull back to the whole chart as the third slide is drawn; hold on the saw-tooth shape
- **SFX:** third descending pluck; a low cold note on "chill"

## c1f
> The authors' reading was that ruling after ruling wears you down, and a tired mind falls back on the easy option. For a parole judge, the easy option is to say no... and leave things as they are.

## c2a
> In nineteen ninety eight, Roy Baumeister and his colleagues ran an experiment that sounds like a prank. Hungry students walked into a lab that smelled of freshly baked chocolate chip cookies.

- **Purpose:** the 1998 experiment set-up: hungry students, a lab smelling of cookies

## c2b

- **Purpose:** the two groups: cookies allowed vs sit in front of them and eat radishes
- **Shot:** pan right; split frame, left group happy with cookies, right group stares at the cookies while holding a radish (the contrast)
- **SFX:** a crunch; a sad crunch

## c2c
> Then everyone got a puzzle. What they weren't told was that it couldn't be solved. The researchers simply timed how long each person kept trying.

- **Purpose:** the unsolvable puzzle and the stopwatch (what was really measured)
- **Shot:** pan right; the puzzle drawn, the hidden "impossible" note revealed, then the stopwatch starts
- **SFX:** puzzle clatter; stopwatch click

## c2d
> The radish eaters gave up after about eight minutes. The cookie eaters kept going for close to twenty.

- **Purpose:** the result: radish eaters quit at ~8 min, cookie eaters ~20 min
- **Shot:** pan right; two bars grow, push on the short radish bar

## c2x

- **Purpose:** the only difference: a few minutes of not eating cookies
- **Shot:** pan right; the two students side by side, identical; one detail circled (the radish)
- **SFX:** pen circle squeak

## c2e
> The idea they proposed was called ego depletion. Self-control runs on a limited resource. Spend some of it resisting cookies, and there's less left for the next hard thing.

- **Purpose:** the theory named: ego depletion, a limited resource; spend some → less left
- **Shot:** pan right; the battery drains in steps as each "hard thing" icon takes a bite
- **SFX:** battery clicks down

## c2f

## c2g

- **Purpose:** the sugar claim: fuel = blood glucose; lemonade tops it up
- **Shot:** pan right; a glass of lemonade is poured, a straw plugs into the battery; push in
