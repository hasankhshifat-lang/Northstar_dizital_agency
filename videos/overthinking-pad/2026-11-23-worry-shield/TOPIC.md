1. **Proposed topic.** Why worry feels productive: Borkovec's definition (mental problem-solving), the perceived functions of
   worry (motivation, preparation, prevention, superstition, distraction from emotion), the 91% untrue-worries study, the
   1990 heart-rate experiment, the contrast avoidance model, the real upside of moderate worry, and three tested things to try.
2. **Why viewers care.** Almost everyone has lain awake running "what ifs" and felt that stopping would be irresponsible.
3. **Central conflict / mystery.** Worry rarely predicts anything (91% false alarms), yet it keeps feeling useful. Why?
4. **Strongest angle.** "Worry isn't a forecast, it's a shield": it really does do something (blunts the body's alarm,
   avoids a sudden emotional drop), just not what we think, and the cost is living braced.
5. **Opening hook.** "It's eleven at night. Tomorrow is the big meeting... What if I freeze?" → "about nine out of ten never
   came true. So why does worry still feel so useful?"
6. **Three titles.**
   - Why Worrying Feels Like Doing Something (It's Not What You Think) (chosen)
   - 91% of Your Worries Never Happen. So Why Does Worry Feel Useful?
   - Your Brain Thinks Worry Is Protecting You. Here's the Catch.
7. **Expected interest.** High: worry/overthinking is a staple search area; the counterintuitive "worry as a shield" reframe
   plus a concrete number is differentiated from generic "stop worrying" tips.
8. **Classification.** Evergreen.
9. **Visual opportunities.** The 11 p.m. bed and the "what if" loop; the definition card with a chain; planning arrow vs
   worry loop; fog vs Tuesday calendar; the rain dance; the 100-square grid (91 crossed); heart-rate traces; the cliff of the
   sudden drop; the two-line drop chart; the car with a foot on the brake; worry appointment clock with a yellow wedge; the
   worry journal; callback to the bed.
10. **Reliable sources.** Borkovec et al. 1983 (BRT, definition); Borkovec, Wilkinson, Folensbee & Lerman 1983 (BRT, stimulus
    control); Borkovec & Roemer 1995 (JBTEP); Stöber 1998 / Stöber & Borkovec 2002 (CTR); Behar et al. 2012 (counter-evidence);
    LaFreniere & Newman 2020 (Behav Ther); Borkovec & Hu 1990 (BRT); Newman & Llera 2011 (Clin Psychol Rev); Llera & Newman
    2010 (Emotion); Llera & Newman 2014 (Behav Ther); Sweeny & Dooley 2017 (SPPC); McGowan & Behar 2013 (Behav Modif);
    Krzikalla et al. 2024 (Clin Psychol Eur); Tallon 2019 (thesis, null result). See FACTS.md.
