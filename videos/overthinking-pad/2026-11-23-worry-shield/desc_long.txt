It's 11 p.m., tomorrow is the big meeting, and your brain is running every "what if". It's exhausting... but it also feels responsible, like you're working on it. Here's the strange part: when people with constant worry wrote down their predictions and checked them for a month, about 91% never came true. So why does worry still feel so useful?

On this page: what worry actually is (Borkovec's definition), why worries stay vague, the reasons worriers give for worrying (and the superstitious loop that keeps it going), the "Exposing worry's deceit" study, the 1990 public-speaking experiment where worry calmed the heart but not the fear, the contrast avoidance model (worry as a shield against a sudden emotional drop), when worry actually helps, and three things researchers have tested: make it concrete, give worry an appointment, keep score.
