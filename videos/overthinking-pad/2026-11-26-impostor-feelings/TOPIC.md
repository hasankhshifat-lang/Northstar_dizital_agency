1. **Proposed topic.** Why capable people feel like frauds: where the idea came from (Clance & Imes 1978), who has it
   (Bravata et al. 2020 review), why success doesn't cure it (the impostor cycle; hidden negative feelings, Jordan et al.
   2011), its surprising upside (Tewfik 2022) and what to try.
2. **Why viewers care.** Students, new hires, anyone who just got good news and felt "they'll find out". Huge search term.
3. **Central question.** If you keep succeeding, why does the "fraud" feeling survive, and is it telling you anything true?
4. **Strongest angle.** Myth-check + twist: it's not a diagnosis; the famous "70%" is shaky (real range 9–82% by measure);
   and impostor thoughts were linked to being rated *better* with people, though with a self-esteem cost.
5. **Opening hook.** Payoff first (EXP-overthinkingpad-002 **treatment #1**): "If you've ever felt like a fraud... here's
   what the research actually says. It's common... rated better with others... not worse." Then the scene (the good-news
   letter and the quiet voice).
6. **Five titles (A2)** — see `metadata.md`.
7. **Expected interest.** High, evergreen; existing videos are mostly opinion/listicles without the newer studies.
8. **Classification.** Evergreen.
9. **Visual opportunities.** The smiling mask held over a worried face; the good-news letter with a gold star; the grey
   "quiet voice" on the shoulder; the diagnostic manual with nothing found; the 20-row questionnaire; the 9–82% ruler and
   the moving cutoff line; the "70%" newspaper with the missing source; the impostor cycle wheel; over-prepare vs put-off
   paths; inside (scribbled head) vs outside (polished cut-outs) and the tipping scale; the filmed doctor + patient,
   gaze lines and nods; the self-esteem gauge; FEELING ≠ BEING; the room with a "don't belong" sign.
10. **Reliable sources.** Clance & Imes 1978 (Psychotherapy: Theory, Research & Practice); Bravata et al. 2020 (J Gen
    Intern Med, systematic review, PubMed 31848865); Sakulku & Alexander 2011 (review of the impostor cycle, Clance 1985);

    et al. 2020 (Frontiers in Psychology, PubMed 33312149); Gravois 2007 (Chronicle of Higher Education) for the 70% origin.
