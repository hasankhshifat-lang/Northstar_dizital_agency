You walk into a room and you're sure everyone noticed: the stain, the trip, the thing you said too loud. In 2000, psychologists at Cornell decided to stop guessing and count. They put students in an embarrassing T-shirt, sent them into a room of other students, and asked how many people noticed. The wearers guessed close to half. The real number was about a quarter.

In this page of notes: the shirt experiment, why the same gap shows up with a shirt people were proud of, the 2002 "good days and bad days" follow-up, why we expect harsher judgment than we get, the 1980 fake-scar study, the illusion of transparency, honest limits of the evidence, and three things to try (name it, widen the frame, count the room).
