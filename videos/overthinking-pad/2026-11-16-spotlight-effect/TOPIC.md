# Topic report: The Spotlight Effect (episode 1, 2026-11-16)

Queue item #1 in `videos/overthinking-pad/topics.md` ("Nobody Noticed Your Shirt"). Research source: YouTube Data
API search (vidIQ is connected but has 4 credits left and each call costs 5, so it couldn't be used this run).

1. **Proposed topic.** The spotlight effect: why we feel watched far more than we are, built around the 2000 Cornell
   "embarrassing T-shirt" experiment and the research that followed (flattering shirts, group discussions, "good
   days and bad days", harsh-judgment fears, the fake-scar study), ending with what actually helps.
2. **Why viewers care.** Almost everyone has walked into a room sure that everyone noticed the stain, the trip, the
   too-loud comment. The video gives them a measured answer to a feeling they live with.
3. **Central conflict / mystery.** The feeling says "everyone saw"; the experiment counted. Why is the gap so big, and
   why doesn't knowing it make the feeling disappear?
4. **Strongest angle.** "Someone actually counted." The shirt study as a story (setup, the guess, the count), then the
   twist that the same gap appears with a shirt people were proud of, so it isn't only about shame: it's about where
   your brain stands when it makes the estimate (anchoring on your own experience).
5. **Opening hook.** "You walk into a room and for one awful second you're certain everyone saw it." Then: in 2000 a
   Cornell team put students in an embarrassing shirt and counted who noticed.
6. **Three titles.**
   - Why You Feel Like Everyone Is Watching You (The Spotlight Effect)
   - The Embarrassing Shirt Experiment: Who Actually Noticed?
   - Nobody Is Watching You As Much As You Think. Here's the Data.
7. **Expected interest.** High. Spotlight-effect Shorts are proven outliers (Zack D. Films "The Spotlight Effect" 9.9M
   views, Mark Manson "Nobody Cares: Beat the Spotlight Effect" 3.1M, Ali Abdaal 476K); long-form explainers from small
   channels reach 80K–155K (Project Better Self 83K, Zenn 155K in 2026). "Why do I replay embarrassing moments"
   is being uploaded weekly by tiny channels with almost no views: demand is there, the well-sourced long version is
   not crowded.
8. **Classification.** Evergreen.
9. **Visual opportunities.** The shirt walk into a room of doodle students; two marker bars (the guess vs the count);
   a spotlight cone that shrinks; a character inside their own "head cinema" (anchoring); the scar study with the
   scar erased by the pen; a meeting with speech bubbles that nobody remembers; the closing "count the room" exercise.
10. **Reliable sources.** Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky 2000 (JPSP); Gilovich, Kruger & Medvec 2002 (JESP); Savitsky,
    Epley & Gilovich 2001 (JPSP); Gilovich, Savitsky & Medvec 1998 (JPSP); Savitsky & Gilovich 2003 (JESP); Kleck &
    Strenta 1980 (JPSP); Brown & Stopa 2007 (J Anxiety Disord). Abstracts via PubMed/Crossref, see FACTS.md.
    Honest limits stated on screen: most of the core studies come from one research group, samples were college
    students, and the exact "half vs a quarter" numbers vary between summaries, so the video gives them as "about".
