It's 2 a.m., you're almost asleep, and your brain plays that thing you said years ago, word for word. Why does a brain that forgets where you left your keys keep a perfect copy of that? And why does replaying it feel useful but often make things worse?

In this page of notes: involuntary memories and what triggers them, why emotional and embarrassing moments stick (Leary's "sociometer"), why the replay hits at night (the Harvard mind-wandering app study, Harvey's model of insomnia), the 1989 earthquake study and the lab experiments on rumination, the 2005 experiment where asking "why" helped only from a distance, concrete "how" vs judging "why me" questions, and three things to try: step back (fly on the wall, talk to yourself by name), swap why-me for how (and write the next step down), and a short absorbing distraction.
