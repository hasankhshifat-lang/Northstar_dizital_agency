# Treatment: "Need 3 Goals" (Barcelona 6–1 PSG, Short, 9:16)

Style: Comic History Cartoon (`comic-history`). 1080×1920, 24 fps, ~50 s. English narration (VibeVoice, Frank),
captions burned in and exported as `.srt`. Script and storyboard: `social/scripts/barca-6-1-psg-remontada.md`.
This Short comes first. The 9-minute long-form reuses its puppets, pitch and scoreboard.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The scoreboard as the protagonist.** One printed aggregate scoreboard sits at the top of the comic page all the way through.
   Every goal is a paper patch slapped onto it, the away-goal rule is a stamp, and the page below changes panel for every
   moment. The tension is in the numbers we can read at a glance.
2. **Two puppets trading blows.** A Barcelona puppet and a PSG puppet face each other over one printed pitch, and each goal
   is a slap from one to the other (the style's "two sides trading blows"). This is fun, but it reduces 22 players to 2 and
   blurs who scored.
3. **The countdown.** A big printed clock fills the frame between panels and the goals happen inside its face. It's tense,
   but it hides the pitch and the players for half the film.

**Choice: 1, with the clock from 3 as a bookend.** The scoreboard makes the maths (needing 3, away goals) visible without a
lecture, which is the whole story. It also turns into the style's native move: patch after patch is laid on the board until it reads 6–5.
The clock opens (88:00) and closes (95:00) the film.

## 2. Logline and arc

Down 4–0 from Paris, and with PSG's away goal meaning they need three more, Barcelona score three times in seven
minutes and the stadium shakes.

- Setup (0–10 s): 88:00, three goals needed; why that's impossible (4–0, the away-goal stamp).
- Turn (10–37 s): 88' free kick, 91' penalty (5–5 but still out), last free kick, the freeze on Sergi Roberto's toe.
- Ending (37–52 s): the board reads 6–5, the pile-on, the seismograph tremor, pull back to the whole page,
  a pin drops on Camp Nou, the comment question.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from** printed sports almanacs and old newspaper match diagrams (engraved pitch plans, small-caps team names,
  hatched stands), and from European comic-strip sports pages (one panel per beat, a fixed score box in the corner).
- **Don't take:** any broadcaster's graphics, club crests, kit sponsors, or real players' faces. Players are generic paper
  puppets in club colours and are identified only by shirt number.

## 4. Shot list (9:16; the comic page is stacked vertically)

Layout: the **scoreboard strip** is pinned at the top (y 120–420), the **action panel** in the middle (y 460–1300), and the **caption band**
at y 1340–1500. The bottom ~400 px stays clear for YouTube's UI.

| # | Time | Framing / angle | Move | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–3.2 | Close insert: printed clock at 88:00 fills the action panel | Slow push in | Hook: time is the enemy |
| 2 | 3.2–10.5 | Close on the scoreboard (FIRST LEG 0–4, a stamp lands: AWAY GOAL ×2 → NEED 3) | Small shake on the stamp | The maths in one image |
| 3 | 10.5–17.0 | Medium, behind #11 at the free kick, the ball's path drawn as a dashed ink line | Push, then a follow along the arc | Hope: the first goal of the burst |
| 4 | 17.0–24.0 | Medium: #9 falls, the referee points, #11 scores the penalty; slip STILL DEBATED | Lateral slide between two small panels | Same minute, two beats |
| 5 | 24.0–32.5 | High angle over the box, every Barcelona puppet runs forward; #11 chips | Slow push in (near silence) | Geography of the last chance |
| 6 | 32.5–36.5 | Freeze: #20's toe and the ball in mid-air, held 1 s | Locked frame, then a big shake on the slap | Payoff: the signature shot |
| 7 | 36.5–45.5 | Full: the pile-on; a narrow corner panel with a seismograph needle scribbling | Shake, then a diagonal drop to the corner panel | Release + true detail |

Signature shot: #6, the freeze on the toe with one second of silence, then the heaviest paper slap of the film (patch "6").

## 5. Beat sheet (to be re-timed to the recorded voice)

- 0.0 tick; clock 88:00. VO: "Barcelona needed three goals... in seven minutes."
- 3.2 whip to the scoreboard; patches 0–4 already on. VO: lost the first leg 4–0... no team had ever come back... an away goal.
- 7.5 stamp AWAY GOAL ×2, slip NEED 3.
- 10.5 88': free kick, net, patch "4" slaps (agg 4–5).
- 17.0 91': penalty awarded, slip STILL DEBATED; patch "5" (5–5); slip PSG THROUGH ON AWAY GOALS.
- 24.0 final seconds: everyone runs forward; short free kick to #11; chip. Music drops to a tremolo.
- 32.5 freeze on #20's toe; 1 s of silence.
- 33.8 slap: patch "6"; board reads 6–5. Crowd + brass.
- 36.5 pile-on; seismograph scribble; slip SMALL TREMOR RECORDED 500 M AWAY.
- 45.5 pull back; pin on Camp Nou; clock 95:00. VO: "Greatest comeback ever... or not even close?"
- 52.0 end.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo 112 bpm (a bar = 2.14 s) for the setup; the brass march accelerates to 132 bpm at 10.5 s (the burst);
everything drops to a solo tremolo at 24.0; silence at 32.5; full band + crowd at 33.8; the music resolves on the pin drop.
Each patch slap lands on a downbeat; the stamp lands on beat 1 of a bar.

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook / maths | low stadium murmur | clock tick, paper slaps, rubber stamp | low brass, sparse |
| The burst (88'–91') | crowd swelling | kick thwack, net rustle, whistle, slaps | brass oom-pah march (Barcelona) vs accordion stabs (PSG) |
| Last chance | crowd hushed | footsteps, heartbeat | thin tremolo only |
| Freeze | silence | — | silence |
| Release | crowd explosion | biggest slap, paper scraps, seismograph scratch | full brass |
| Ending | crowd fading | pin piercing paper | brass resolves; a glockenspiel button |

## 8. Captions and titles

- Narration captions in pale yellow caption boxes (#F5E9C4, ink border) in the caption band, English only, one or two lines.
  Hold ≥ max(1.8 s, speech + 0.6 s).
- Numbers in Old Standard TT (lining figures); labels and slips in IM FELL English SC; the hook text in Lilita One.
- Scoreboard: BARCELONA in printed blue, PSG in printed navy; patches one step lighter than their owner's colour.
- No club crests, no league marks, no broadcaster graphics. Players' names appear only in the narration and captions.
