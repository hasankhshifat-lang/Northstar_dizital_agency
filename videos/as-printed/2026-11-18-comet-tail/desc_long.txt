In February 1910, American newspapers reported that astronomers had found cyanogen, "perhaps the deadliest poison known," in the tail of Halley's comet, and that the Earth would pass through that tail in May. Papers spread the claim that the gas "may snuff out life upon the earth," comet pills went on sale, and on the night of May 18 the world waited. What happened next is stranger than the scare: the instruments recorded nothing, and the astronomers could not even agree whether the Earth had touched the tail at all. Told from the real pages, February–May 1910.
