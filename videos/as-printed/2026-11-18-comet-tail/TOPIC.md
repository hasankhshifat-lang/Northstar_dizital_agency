# Topic research report — "The Poison Tail" (As Printed, episode 4)

Per docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md §2.G–H. Topic 4 in `videos/as-printed/topics.md` (Halley's Comet panic, May 1910).

1. **Topic:** in February 1910 papers reported that Yerkes Observatory had found cyanogen, "perhaps the deadliest poison
   known", in the tail of Halley's comet, and that the Earth would pass through the tail in May; the claim that the gas
   "may snuff out life upon the earth" was pinned on Camille Flammarion; comet pills were sold. On 18 May nothing was
   measured, and the next day astronomers could not agree whether the Earth had touched the tail at all.
2. **Why viewers care:** an end-of-the-world scare with a real scientific seed; a media-panic story that still feels
   modern; Halley's comet is famous.
3. **Central question:** what really happened on the night the Earth went through the poison tail?
4. **Strongest angle:** the archive answers itself: Flammarion's own letter (LA Herald, 2 Feb) already said the tail
   "will be so rarified as to be inoffensive", a week before the "snuff out life" headlines; and on 19–20 May the papers
   printed both "World passes safely through tail" and Hale's "I do not know whether the earth passed through the tail".
5. **Hook:** "a New York newspaper told its readers that astronomers had found poison in the sky … in the tail of a
   comet. And in May, the Earth was going to pass right through that tail."
6. **Titles:** "The Comet That Was Supposed to Gas the Earth (1910)" · "1910: The Papers Said a Comet Would Poison Us" ·
   "The Night Earth Flew Through a Poison Comet Tail"
7. **Audience interest:** high (doomsday + science + press panic; comment question on who is to blame).
8. **Evergreen / trending:** evergreen.
9. **Visuals:** 11 real pages Feb–May 1910 (headlines lit under the loupe); 2 photographs (Yerkes Observatory panorama
   c1910; Lick 36-inch telescope stereograph 1902); 3 period illustrations (Harper's Weekly cover 21 May 1910, Harper's
   4 June 1910, Illustrated London News 28 May 1910).
10. **Sources:** Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Prints & Photographs). See FACTS.md.
11. **Score:** curiosity 18/20 · emotion 11/15 · suspense 12/15 · demand 13/15 · unexpected 9/10 · visual 8/10 ·
    discussion 9/10 · research 5/5 = **85/100** (excellent).

Rotation: episode 1 disaster (Titanic), 2 war/press, 3 famous person/misquote, 4 science scare: no disaster, new era.

(Earth will pass through it) and the question (what happened that night), with the promise that the astronomers
couldn't even agree, without revealing the answer; each part ends on a loop; every part adds new items; the turn is
Flammarion's own "inoffensive" line (Part 2, mid-video); the second turn is Topeka/Bisbee (Part 5); the verdict is the
compare shot; one comment question and one short like/subscribe line at the end; every quote is in FACTS.md.
