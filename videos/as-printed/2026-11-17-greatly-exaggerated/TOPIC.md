# Topic research report — "At Death's Door" (As Printed, episode 3)

Per docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md §2.G–H. Topic 3 in `videos/as-printed/topics.md` (Mark Twain's death, 1897),
widened to the three false death reports (May 1897, June 1897, May 1907) and the real one (April 1910).

1. **Topic:** the papers reported Mark Twain dead (early May 1897), dying in poverty (31 May 1897) and lost at sea (4 May
   1907) while he was alive; his own reply, as printed in the New York Journal on 2 June 1897, was "The report of my
   death was an exaggeration." — and by 1907 a paper was already "repeating" it as "the reports of my death have been
   greatly exaggerated."
2. **Why viewers care:** everyone knows the quote; almost nobody knows it is a later polish. Twain is the most famous
   American writer; "the papers got it wrong, then rewrote the correction" is the channel promise exactly.
3. **Central question:** how does a famous man keep being reported dead, and what did he really say?
4. **Strongest angle:** the misquote is documented inside the archive itself (1897 Journal vs 1907 Washington Times).
5. **Hook:** "Mark Twain at death's door." (Evening Times, 31 May 1897) → "the words you know are not the words he gave."
6. **Titles:** "The Papers Kept Killing Mark Twain" · "Mark Twain's Famous Quote Isn't What He Said" ·
   "How Newspapers Rewrote Mark Twain's Best Line"
7. **Audience interest:** high (Twain + famous quote + misinformation; comment question invites opinions).
8. **Evergreen / trending:** evergreen.
9. **Visuals:** 11 real pages 1896–1910 (headlines lit under the loupe), 5 LoC photographs including Twain reading a
   newspaper (Bolles c1902), the yacht Kanawha (1897), Rogers and Clemens (Bain), Twain 1907 (Bradley), Twain 1910 (Bain).
10. **Sources:** Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Prints & Photographs). See FACTS.md.
11. **Score:** curiosity 18/20 · emotion 11/15 · suspense 12/15 · demand 13/15 · unexpected 9/10 · visual 8/10 ·
    discussion 9/10 · research 5/5 = **85/100** (excellent).
