He had then reached London, after the death of his daughter, in a terribly depressed condition and in bad health". Agreeing (same AP text): Topeka State Journal 1 June 1897 p. 1 (prints the date as "May 8"; the narration uses the Star's "May fifth" and only says "early May" elsewhere) | https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1897-06-01/ed-1/?sp=2 ; https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82016014/1897-06-01/ed-1/?sp=1 |

The above cablegram effectually disposes of the rumor current in this city yesterday that the well-known writer was dying in poverty in England." (the narration dates the rumor to Monday 31 May, the day before this issue, which agrees with the Evening Times of 31 May) | https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030180/1897-06-01/ed-1/?sp=1 |
| New York Journal and Advertiser, p. 1 | Wed. 2 June 1897 | "MARK TWAIN AMUSED."; "Humorist Says He Even Heard on Good Authority That He Was Dead."; "COUSIN, NOT HE, SICK."; "By Frank Marshall White."; "London, June 1.—Mark Twain was undecided whether to be more amused or annoyed when a Journal representative informed him to-day of the report in New York that he was dying in poverty in London. He is living in comfort and even luxury in a handsomely furnished house in a beautiful square in Chelsea with his wife and children"; "He only to-day settled finally upon the title, which is to be \"Another Innocent Abroad.\""; quote: "I can understand perfectly how the report of my illness got about. I have even heard on good authority that I was dead. James Ross Clemens, of St. Louis, a cousin of mine, was seriously ill two or three weeks ago in London, but is well now. The report of my illness grew out of his illness. The report of my death was an exaggeration." (8 words) | https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030180/1897-06-02/ed-1/?sp=1 |

H. Rogers's Kanawha, having on board Mark Twain. The yacht has been missing since Wednesday"; "The Kanawha was marooned several days in the Roads. Mr. Rogers and his son went north by rail. Mr. Clemens declined to go by rail, and remained on board the Kanawha. He fretted considerably, as the fog would not lift." | https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030214/1907-05-04/ed-1/?sp=1 |

| The Washington Times, Last Edition, p. 6 | Sat. evening 4 May 1907 | "Story of His Death Again Exaggerated Greatly."; "NEW YORK, May 4.—Mark Twain had the pleasure today of repeating his celebrated statement that \"the reports of my death have been greatly exaggerated.\""; "Mr. Clemens read the story of the disaster which had befallen him at his Fifth avenue home, and enjoyed it hugely."; box: "Still a Little Foggy. That Actors' Fund affair is still a little foggy, but I'm not lost in it. Neither am I aboard Mr. Rogers' yacht. I'm simply paddling my own canoe.—Mark Twain." | https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84026749/1907-05-04/ed-1/?sp=6 |
