In the spring of 1897, American newspapers reported Mark Twain dead, then dying of a broken heart in a London lodging house. He was alive, and his answer to the New York Journal became the most famous correction ever printed. But the words most people quote are not the words he gave: ten years later, when the papers lost him at sea, they were already "repeating" his line in a new, polished form. This is the story told from the real front pages, 1896–1910.
