# Topic research report — "The Coupon King" (As Printed, episode 6)

Per docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md §2.G–H. Topic 10 in `videos/as-printed/topics.md` (Charles Ponzi, 1920),
taken ahead of topic 6 (1918 flu) on purpose: the previous episode was the USS Maine explosion, and the runbook says
never two disasters in a row. The flu stays first in the queue for the next episode.

1. **Topic:** on 31 July 1920 the New-York Tribune front page called Charles Ponzi "the financial wizard", "said to
   have made more than $9,000,000 in a few months by dealing in international postal coupons". The same story, jumped
   to page 3, quoted the Boston postmaster: the post office had redeemed "only $30 worth" of reply coupons for Ponzi's
   company in three months. Ponzi kept paying out for nine more days; arrested 12 Aug (30,000 investors, ~$20 million);
   pleaded guilty 30 Nov 1920.
2. **Why viewers care:** "Ponzi scheme" is a household phrase (Madoff, crypto collapses) but few know the story or
   the coupon trick; money, greed, a charming con man, a crowd that cheered him during the run.
3. **Central question:** how did one man keep a whole city believing, and what was the number that should have stopped it?
4. **Strongest angle:** the contradiction is printed under one headline ($9,000,000 vs $30), plus "he paid" as the
   reason the crowd believed.
5. **Hook:** "In the summer of 1920, a New York front page called him the financial wizard. Nine million dollars, in a
   few months. From postage coupons."
6. **Titles:** "How Charles Ponzi Fooled Boston With Postage Coupons (1920)" · "The Ponzi Number the Papers Printed and
   Missed" · "The Original Ponzi Scheme, As the Papers Printed It"
7. **Audience interest:** high (evergreen money/fraud topic, strong search demand for "Ponzi"; comment question:
   would you have taken your money out or waited).
8. **Evergreen / trending:** evergreen.
9. **Visuals:** 9 real pages July–Nov 1920 (headlines lit under the loupe); 3 photographs (Ponzi, Bain News Service;
   Post Office Square, Boston; Boston City Hall, School Street panorama).
10. **Sources:** Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Prints & Photographs); Pulitzer Prize records; see FACTS.md.
11. **Score:** curiosity 17/20 · emotion 12/15 · suspense 12/15 · demand 15/15 · unexpected 9/10 · visual 8/10 ·
    discussion 9/10 · research 5/5 = **87/100** (excellent).

Rotation: ep. 1 disaster (Titanic), 2 war/press, 3 famous person, 4 science scare, 5 war origin, 6 money/crime.

Script check (§15): first sentence is the surprise (the "financial wizard", $9 million from postage coupons); first
30 s give the claim, the conflict (a post office number on the same page) and the question, without revealing the
$30; parts end on loops ("count the coupons", "where was the money coming from?", "the window was about to close");
