## Facts and quotations

| Claim in the film | Source and printed wording |
|---|---|
| A New York front page called Ponzi "the financial wizard", "said to have made more than $9,000,000 in a few months by dealing in international postal coupons" | trib31 p. 1: "U. S. Searches For Mystery In Ponzi Coup … New troubles are piling up for Charles Ponzi, the financial wizard, who is said to have made more than $9,000,000 in a few months by dealing in international postal coupons." |
| Boston post office: only $30 of coupons in three months | trib31b p. 3 (same story): "[A state]ment came from Postmaster Roland Baker that the Boston post office redeemed only $30 worth of International Postal Reply coupons in the last three months for Ponzi's exchange company." |
| US redemptions to June 30, 1918: $54,436.69 | trib31b, U.S. District Attorney Gallagher: "Up to and including the year ending June 30, 1918, $54,436.69 worth of coupons were redeemed by this government." Business taken up "since 1908" → "about ten years". |
| "Nine more days" of payments after 31 July | Payments continued (wtimes3, 3 Aug: "Payments were resumed as usual"); trib10 (dateline 9 Aug): Ponzi "would 'not pay out one cent of money' to-morrow". 31 Jul → 9 Aug = 9 days. |
| Headline "Makes Dollars Gain 50 Cents Within 54 Days"; 45 days further down | azrep: headline as quoted (scan reads "54"); body: "Invariably Ponzi is said to have paid off the note in 45 days"; "This usually takes 45 days, according to Ponzi, who says his profits have reached 400 per cent in some transactions." |
| Crowds, 50% payable in 90 days, "no complaint" | azrep: "For several weeks crowds have flocked to Ponzi's offices and given their savings in exchange for notes of the company for the principal plus 50 per cent payable in 90 days … there has been no complaint that any person has failed to receive money when due." |
| Reply-coupon system | azrep: "Ponzi describes his exchange system as being based on the use of the international reply coupon authorized under the international postal agreement"; converts dollars into lire, coupons "redeemable at the normal and not the prevailing rate of exchange". How a reply coupon works (exchangeable abroad for postage): Universal Postal Union / https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_reply_coupon |
| Securities Exchange Company | azrep, trib28 ("head of the Securities Exchange Company") |
| 26 July: deposits halted pending audit | azrep (Boston, 26 July): "A bar was placed today on the rush of people to give their money to Charles Ponzi … agreed to accept no further deposits until an auditor selected by the district attorney has examined his accounts, which are said to total millions." |
| Offices near City Hall, Pie Alley entrance | wtimes3: "lined up outside the Pie alley entrance to the office of the Securities Exchange Company … a long line which extended out to City Hall avenue"; wtimes2: "The throng began to gather early in Pie alley". |
| "Money Wizard Pays All Claims; Court Denies Injunction"; $10 to $50,000 | trib28: headline; "Thousands of persons in all stations of life have invested money with Ponzi, ranging from $10 to $50,000. The notes call for payment of an increment of 50 per cent … within ninety days, but in most instances Ponzi has paid principal and profit in full in that time." |
| "I should worry" | trib31: "But the dapper little man who has them all guessing continues to smile, nonchalantly signing thousands of dollars' worth of checks … and declaring, 'I should worry.'" |
| McMasters charges, 2 Aug | wtimes2: "Claims Ponzi Is Insolvent … Charges that Ponzi is 'unbalanced on one subject, is hopelessly insolvent, has not sufficient funds to meet his notes, and has sent or received no money from Europe recently' … written by William H. McMasters, who has been employed as a publicity man by Ponzi." The paper names "a morning newspaper"; it was the Boston Post (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ponzi ; Poynter, "What journalism owes to Charles Ponzi", 2009). |
| Ponzi's denial | wtimes2: "McMasters' statement that I have not sent money abroad and that it has not taken the course of my system of operations and returned to this country with heavy profits is entirely false." |
| The run, 500+ note-holders, cheer | wtimes3 (Boston, 3 Aug): "Pays Millions to Investors … More than 500 note-holders, anxious to get their money back, lined up …"; "A cheer went up from the waiting men and women when the doors were opened". |
| 10 Aug: bankruptcy, Hanover Trust order | trib10: "Ponzi Sued As Bankrupt By Creditors … involuntary bankruptcy proceedings had been instituted against Ponzi by three creditors and Joseph C. Allen, State Bank Commissioner, had issued an order to the Hanover Trust Company not to pay out any more money on checks of Ponzi or his agents." |
| 13 Aug: arrest; 30,000 persons, $20,000,000; "stupendous fraud"; under $500,000 in six years | trib13: headline "Ponzi Is Arrested; Took in 20 Millions; Says He Cannot Pay"; "Financial Wizard Said To Be 'Hopelessly Insolvent' by Officials Who Scanned Accounts"; "An estimate that some 30,000 persons intrusted upward of $20,000,000 to the promoter"; "A declaration of a Postoffice inspector that Ponzi's claim that he had made millions by manipulating international reply coupons was 'a stupendous fraud' and that in six years dealings in those coupons throughout the international postal union would not aggregate $500,000." |
| Hanover Trust closed, Ponzi a director | trib13: "the capital of the Hanover Trust Company, of which Ponzi was a director, was not only seriously impaired, but probably wiped out"; side story: "before the closing of the institution". |
| 30 Nov 1920: guilty plea, five years | star30: "Ponzi Gets 5-Year Term For Fraud. Promoter Changes Plea to Guilty — 'Thus Passes Glory,' He Writes … pleaded guilty to using the mails in a scheme to defraud … Sentence of five years in the Plymouth county jail … charged that Ponzi had represented falsely that he was able to pay interest at the rate of 50 per cent in forty-five days from profits made in international postal reply coupons." |
| "House of easy money … props of income from investors" | star30: "The little Italian who, with prison sentences in Canada and this country behind him, developed his supposed scheme … only to have his house of easy money topple over on him when the props of income from investors were pulled out." |
| "Sic transit gloria mundi" | star30: "he penciled on a memorandum block his parting word to the public: 'Sic transit gloria mundi' (Thus passes the glory of the world)". |
| Early investors paid with later investors' money | star30 (above) + federal charge; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ponzi ; EBSCO Research Starters, "Ponzi Schemes Are Revealed as Investment Frauds". |
| Deported to Italy 1934; died Rio de Janeiro 1949, nearly penniless | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ponzi ; Time (1949 obituary, content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,794507,00.html: Rio charity ward); EBSCO Research Starters. |
| Boston Post won the 1921 Pulitzer Prize for Public Service for exposing Ponzi | https://www.pulitzer.org/winners/boston-post ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1921_Pulitzer_Prize |
| Ponzi "an Italian immigrant in Boston" | star30 ("The little Italian"); Wikipedia. |
