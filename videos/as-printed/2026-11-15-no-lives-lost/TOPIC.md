# Topic research report — "No Lives Lost" (As Printed, episode 1)

Per docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md §2.G–H.

1. **Topic:** the Titanic as America read it on 15–16 April 1912: Monday's "no lives lost" front pages, Tuesday's correction.
2. **Why viewers care:** the Titanic is one of the most-watched history subjects (23 of 241 English 1M+ history Shorts in
   the 2026-11-08 research were Titanic); this angle is fresh: not the sinking itself, but the evening America believed everyone lived.
3. **Central mystery/conflict:** how did front pages in several cities print a rescue that never happened, and what is the real count?
4. **Strongest angle:** the same Monday, papers disagreed (afloat / all saved / sinking); Tuesday the Virginian's own captain killed the story.
5. **Opening hook:** "Everyone is safe." — what families read that evening, while the ship was already on the bottom.
6. **Titles:** "The Night America Read 'No Lives Lost'" · "Titanic: The Front Page That Got It All Wrong" · "Why Newspapers Said Everyone Survived the Titanic"
7. **Audience interest:** high (evergreen Titanic demand; misinformation angle invites comments).
8. **Evergreen / trending:** evergreen.
9. **Visuals:** seven real front pages (headlines lit under the loupe), five Bain glass negatives (crowds, lifeboats, survivors, Rostron).
10. **Sources:** Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Prints & Photographs), National Archives Prologue (2012). See FACTS.md.
11. **Score:** curiosity 18/20 · emotion 13/15 · suspense 11/15 · demand 14/15 · unexpected 8/10 · visual 9/10 · discussion 8/10 · research 5/5 = **86/100** (excellent).

Script check (§15): first sentence is the surprise; the first 30 s set the conflict and the question; loops at the end of the
cold open and each chapter; each chapter adds a new item; the turn (Tuesday's wire) before the verdict; payoff answers the
opening; one comment question and one short like/subscribe line at the end; every number is in FACTS.md.
