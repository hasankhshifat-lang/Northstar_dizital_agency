## The record

| Claim | Source | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Iceberg sighted 11:40 p.m. on 14 April; the ship sank less than three hours later | National Archives, Prologue, Spring 2012 — https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2012/spring/titanic.html | "sank less than three hours after the crew spotted an iceberg at 11:40 p.m. on April 14." |
| The Carpathia picked up 706 people from the lifeboats on the morning of 15 April | NARA, same | "the 706 persons it picked up from Titanic's lifeboats on the morning of April 15, 1912" |
| The Carpathia landed its passengers in New York on 18 April | NARA, same | "when the Carpathia landed its passengers [at the port of New York on April 18]" |
| 2,223 aboard, 1,517 died | NARA, same | "Of the 2,223 people aboard, 1,517 perished." |

Wording choices: the film says the ship "struck an iceberg at 11:40" only as "the iceberg was sighted at 11:40"
(NARA's wording). It does not explain where the false reports came from beyond what the pages show (wireless
reports, contradictory on the same day). It says the papers printed "what came over the wire" as interpretation,
not as a documented fact about any one paper.
