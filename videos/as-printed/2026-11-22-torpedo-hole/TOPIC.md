# Topic research report — "The Torpedo Hole" (As Printed, episode 5)

Per docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md §2.G–H. Topic 5 in `videos/as-printed/topics.md` (USS Maine, February 1898).

1. **Topic:** two days after the Maine exploded in Havana harbor (15 Feb 1898), Hearst's New York Evening Journal
   printed "TORPEDO HOLE DISCOVERED BY GOVERNMENT DIVERS IN THE MAINE" — an "8-inch percussion hole", "Spanish
   treachery". The same front page says the court of inquiry had only just been named and "[di]vers will go down
   to-day at the wreck". The 1898 court found a mine but "no evidence … fixing the responsibility"; the 1911 board put
   the mine somewhere else; the Navy's 1976 Rickover study found "no evidence that a mine destroyed the Maine".
2. **Why viewers care:** "Remember the Maine" is famous; a front page that convicted an enemy before the evidence
   existed, and a war that followed in ten weeks; the cause is still argued.
3. **Central question:** who found the torpedo hole, and what really sank the Maine?
4. **Strongest angle:** the contradiction is on the same front page (torpedo hole found / divers go down today), plus
   the captain's own telegram: "Public opinion should be suspended till further report."
5. **Hook:** "Two days after the battleship Maine blew up in Havana harbor, a New York paper told its readers that
   divers had found the hole."
6. **Titles:** "The Torpedo Hole That Started a War (USS Maine, 1898)" · "Divers Found a Torpedo Hole. They Hadn't Gone Down Yet" ·
   "What Really Sank the USS Maine? The Papers Knew in 2 Days"
7. **Audience interest:** high (war origin, media blame, unsolved cause; comment question on whether waiting would have changed the war).
8. **Evergreen / trending:** evergreen.
9. **Visuals:** 8 real pages Feb 1898 – Dec 1911 (headlines lit under the loupe); 4 photographs (two Griffith & Griffith
   diver stereographs 1898; two 1911 cofferdam panoramas); 2 period prints (the Maine; Capt. Sigsbee).
10. **Sources:** Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Prints & Photographs); Rickover 1976 (US Navy). See FACTS.md.
11. **Score:** curiosity 18/20 · emotion 12/15 · suspense 12/15 · demand 14/15 · unexpected 9/10 · visual 8/10 ·
    discussion 9/10 · research 5/5 = **87/100** (excellent).

Rotation: ep. 1 disaster (Titanic), 2 war/press, 3 famous person, 4 science scare, 5 war origin/press — not two
disasters in a row (previous was the comet scare).
