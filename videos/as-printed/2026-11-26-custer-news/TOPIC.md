# Topic research report — "Read It in the Papers" (As Printed, episode 8)

Per docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md §2.G–H. Queue topic 8 ("Custer, June–July 1876: first reports of the Little
Bighorn → the army count"). The previous episode was the 1918 flu (public health) and #7/#11/#13 are disasters, so
this is the open topic that avoids two disasters in a row. Demand (A1): `youtube-growth/asprinted/research/
topic-opportunities/2026-11-26.md` (YouTube API fallback; vidIQ out of credits).

1. **Topic:** On 6 July 1876 the papers printed Custer's death (New-York Tribune: "every man killed, 315 in number",
   from a Bozeman dispatch of 3 July). The army's two most senior generals, in Philadelphia for the Centennial week,
   told the New York Herald they had no news, that it was "so incredible that he could not believe it" (Sheridan) and
   "It must be exaggerated" (Sherman), until a telegram arrived during Sherman's interview. In Washington the War
   Department was "without advices"; the army's own telegram said Terry's dispatch of 2 July "confirm[s] the newspaper
   reports". The record: Terry's dispatch (via steamboat to Bismarck) and today's NPS count (268 killed).
2. **Why viewers care:** Little Bighorn is one of the most watched American history topics; almost nobody knows the
   army learned it from the papers, two days after the Centennial Fourth.
3. **Central question:** How did a newspaper know before the generals, and who was right when they disagreed?
4. **Strongest angle:** Two news roads: a civilian scout who reached a telegraph in Bozeman (3 July) against the army
   report that went by steamboat to Bismarck (5 July night). The generals trusted the slow road and doubted the fast
   one. Fair to them: the printed toll (315) really was too high.
5. **Hook:** "On the morning of July sixth, 1876, General Philip Sheridan found out that Custer was dead. He read it in
   the newspaper. Like everyone else." → Herald line "read the painful narrative in the morning papers".
6. **Titles (A2):** see metadata.md (5 candidates + pick).
7. **Audience interest:** high (Custer/Little Bighorn long-form demand); comment question: would you have believed
   "every man killed" with no word from the army?
8. **Evergreen / trending:** evergreen; 150th anniversary year of the battle (1876 → 2026).
9. **Visuals:** 8 real pages (New York Herald, New-York Tribune ×3, National Republican, Evening Star, Helena Weekly
   Herald, Bozeman Avant Courier), 7 photographs/prints (Custer, Sheridan, Sherman, Sitting Bull, Centennial Main
   Building, S. J. Morrow's camp at the ford and head-boards). Non-graphic throughout.
10. **Sources:** Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Prints & Photographs, "no known restrictions"), National
    Park Service (Little Bighorn Battlefield chronology and casualty figures), see FACTS.md.

    discussion 8/10 · research 5/5 = **87/100** (strong).
