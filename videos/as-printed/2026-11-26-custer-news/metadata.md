## Long description
Custer's death at the Little Bighorn reached America in July 1876 through the newspapers, and the U.S. Army's own generals read it there first.
General Sheridan called the story "so incredible that he could not believe it"; General Sherman said "It must be exaggerated", until a telegram came to his door.

On 6 July 1876 the New-York Tribune printed "Gen. Custer killed … every man killed, 315 in number", from a dispatch a civilian scout had carried to the telegraph in Bozeman, Montana, on 3 July. The army's own report was still on a steamboat to Bismarck. We read the pages themselves: the Herald's interviews with Sheridan and Sherman in Philadelphia during the Centennial week, the War Department "without advices", and the army telegram that "confirm[s] the newspaper reports". Then we check the printed toll against the record.
