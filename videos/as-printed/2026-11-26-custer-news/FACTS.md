## Context facts (two sources each)

| Fact (as narrated) | Sources |
|---|---|
| Battle 25 June 1876; 7th Cavalry attacked a large Lakota and Northern Cheyenne village on the Little Bighorn; the defenders won | NPS chronology https://www.nps.gov/libi/learn/a-chronology-of-the-battle-of-the-little-bighorn.htm ; Tribune/Herald 6–7 July (date 25 June) |
| 268 soldiers, civilians and Indian scouts killed or mortally wounded; Custer's five companies (C, E, F, I, L) about 210 men, none survived; Lakota/Cheyenne losses estimated ~30 to ~100 (published figures vary) | NPS chronology (268; "an estimated 60-100 Lakota and Cheyenne"); NPS history (https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/hh/1b/hh1j.htm: about 210 with Custer); History.com citing NPS (30–100). Narration says "between thirty and about a hundred" to cover the range |
| Far West reached Bismarck late on 5 July with the news and the wounded; Bismarck Tribune published 6 July | NPS chronology; Wikipedia "Mark Kellogg (reporter)" (news reached Bismarck 5 July, special issue next day); Evening Star 7 Jul (wounded carried to the steamboat; dispatch sent to Bismarck) |
| Route of the boat: Bighorn → Yellowstone → Missouri | geography of the Far West's run (NPS chronology; Star: steamboat "in the Yellowstone") |
| Taylor reached a telegraph about two days before the boat: Bozeman 3 July evening vs Bismarck late 5 July | Tribune 6 Jul + Avant Courier 7 Jul (3 July); NPS (5 July) |
| Mark Kellogg, reporting for the Bismarck Tribune, rode with Custer's wing and was killed 25 June 1876 | Wikipedia "Mark Kellogg (reporter)"; NPS/HistoryNet (https://historynet.com/correspondent-mark-kellogg-death-little-bighorn/) |
| Sheridan commanded the Military Division of the Missouri (campaign); Sherman was Commanding General of the Army; both in Philadelphia | National Republican 7 Jul ("Gen. Sheridan's headquarters"; "Secretary Cameron, General Sherman and Lieutenant General Sheridan were in Philadelphia, in attendance upon the meeting of the Army of the Cumberland"); Herald 7 Jul; standard biographies |
| Custer born 5 Dec 1839 → 36 in June 1876; brigadier general of volunteers in June 1863 at 23 | LoC portrait records ("1839-1876"); Wikipedia "George Armstrong Custer"; NPS |
| Sitting Bull, Hunkpapa Lakota leader, was in the village | LoC record 99402422 / LC-USZ62-122859 ("Sitting Bull", c1884); NPS |
