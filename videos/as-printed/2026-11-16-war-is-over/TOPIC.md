# Topic research report — "War Is Over!" (As Printed, episode 2)

Per docs/content/scriptwriting-rules.md §2.G–H. Topic 2 in `videos/as-printed/topics.md` (the False Armistice).

1. **Topic:** the False Armistice of Thursday, 7 November 1918: a United Press bulletin from Brest, France said the
   armistice had been signed; papers from Seattle to Indiana printed "WAR IS OVER!"; the State Department denied it the
   same afternoon; the real armistice was signed at 5 a.m. on Monday, 11 November.
2. **Why viewers care:** Veterans Day / Armistice Day just passed (11 Nov); WWI is evergreen; "fake news a century ago"
   is a strong, current angle; the story has a clear villain-less mystery (who sent it?) and a clean payoff.
3. **Central mystery/conflict:** how did a signed bulletin from the president of a major news wire announce a peace that
   didn't exist, and where did it come from?
4. **Strongest angle:** the doubt was already printed on the same front pages as the celebration (Lake County Times:
   "WAR ENDS" + "STATE DEPT. HAD NO CONFIRMATION AT 2 P.M."); then the chain traced back to a U.S. admiral's office.
5. **Opening hook:** "War is over." — the Seattle Star, Thursday 7 Nov 1918; it went on for four more days.
6. **Titles:** "The Day America Celebrated a Peace That Didn't Exist" · "War Is Over! (Four Days Too Soon)" ·
   "How a Signed Bulletin Ended WWI Four Days Early"
7. **Audience interest:** high (WWI + misinformation; misspelled headline and admiral's confession invite comments).
8. **Evergreen / trending:** evergreen, with an Armistice/Veterans Day tail this week.
9. **Visuals:** eleven real front pages 7–11 Nov 1918 (headlines lit under the loupe, the misspelled "ARMISITCE"),
   Harris & Ewing portraits of Roy W. Howard and Adm. Henry B. Wilson, three Bain "Peace Hurrah" negatives of 11 Nov 1918.
   No photographs of the 7 Nov celebrations were found in LoC P&P with clear rights, so the film shows the 7 Nov crowds
   only through the printed reports and uses the 11 Nov photographs only where it says they are from 11 Nov.
10. **Sources:** Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Prints & Photographs), Foreign Relations of the United States
    1918 (Office of the Historian, State Department). See FACTS.md.
11. **Score:** curiosity 18/20 · emotion 12/15 · suspense 12/15 · demand 12/15 · unexpected 9/10 · visual 9/10 ·
    discussion 8/10 · research 5/5 = **85/100** (excellent).

Rotation check: episode 1 was a disaster (Titanic); this one is war/press, not a disaster.
