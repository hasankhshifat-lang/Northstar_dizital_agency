# TREATMENT — "No Lives Lost" (the Titanic, April 1912)

Archive Light Table · YouTube Short · 1080×1920 · 24 fps · ~50 s · English narration + burned-in subtitles + .srt

## 1. Three candidate structures

- **A. The ship's last day, in order.** Departure → iceberg → sinking → rescue. Everyone knows it; the
  archive only illustrates it, and the light table has no question to answer.
- **B. The count.** One number, read off the papers as the truth arrives: Monday evening "no lives lost" (0) →
  Tuesday morning 1,340 → Tuesday 1,492 → the National Archives' record, 1,517. The loupe reads each number off
  its page; the photographs show what the numbers stood for: the crowd waiting for news, the lifeboats.
- **C. Two photographs.** Only the White Star office crowd and the lifeboats, before/after. Strong pictures, but
  no claim to test, so no turn.

**Chosen: B.** It is a claim and its correction, exactly what the light table does: a headline lights up, a
number lifts off the page, the archive's record answers it. The hook is a sentence everyone knows is wrong ("No
lives lost"), the turn is that the ship had already sunk hours before the paper was printed, and the ending is
the number struck and replaced, held still.

## 2. Logline and arc

On the evening of 15 April 1912 a New Haven paper told its readers the Titanic was afloat and no one had died.
The ship had sunk before dawn. Over the next day the papers' number climbed; the record says 1,517.

Setup: the "no lives lost" headline → Turn: it had sunk hours earlier; New York waits at the White Star office;
the next morning, 1,340, then 1,492 → Peak: inside the lifeboat photograph, 706 picked up → Ending: 0 struck,
1,517 held; the table becomes the index.

## 3. Benchmark

- Archive documentaries that push into still photographs (learn: let a photo hold, move inside it slowly; one
  photograph = one feeling). Don't take: their pacing, music, type.
- Visual investigations on news video (learn: one line, one reading, one source per screen; quote the original
  wording). Don't take: their layouts or graphics.

## 6. Cue map

Tempo 72 bpm (bar = 3.333 s), 4/4, D minor. Quiet and modern, no period pastiche.
- **Table on** (0–1.5): starter click and the fluorescent hum; one low piano note on the flicker.
- **The claim** (1.5–8.5): sparse piano, a felt-muted pulse on quarters; a soft tick each time a line lights.
- **The crowd** (8.5–15): pulse continues, a low string pad enters with the negative turning positive.
- **The turn** (15–19.5): pulse stops; a single sustained low note under "sank before dawn".
- **The count** (19.5–27): pulse returns a step higher; a click per counter step.
- **Signature** (27–37): riser under the push, sound sucked out at the threshold, then sea wind and a full chord
  inside the photograph; strings very quiet.
- **Verdict** (37–44): two bars nearly silent; the strike lands on a low D.
- **Index** (44–50): piano figure resolves, hum fades, end.

## 8. Type, colour, sources

- Table #F7F9FB, ink #15171A, one signal colour (orange) for marks, readings and the strike; caption plates dark.
- Monospace for catalogue numbers, dates and readings; a sans for labels; a serif only for the title card.
- Every object carries its holder's identifier (Chronicling America LCCN + date, or LC-B2 negative number).
- Subtitles: a dark strip with a signal-orange edge, bottom centre, above YouTube's bottom ~400 px.
- Credits line on the end card: "Library of Congress (Chronicling America; Bain News Service negatives) · National Archives".
