### Facts used (2+ sources agree)
- Chicago sat barely above Lake Michigan on marshy ground, poor drainage, water standing in the streets (Wikipedia, WBEZ).
- 1854 cholera killed about 6% / "1 in 20" (Wikipedia, WBEZ).
- From 1856: sewers laid, covered with feet of soil, buildings raised to the new grade on jackscrews (Wikipedia, WBEZ).
- Lake Street block 1860: 600 men, 6,000 jackscrews, business went on as usual (Wikipedia, WBEZ, Gizmodo); raised 4 ft 8 in (Wikipedia) → "more than four feet" (Gizmodo title).

## Script (136 words, VibeVoice: numbers as words, no tags)

In eighteen sixty, Chicago lifted an entire city block... while the shops inside stayed open. Why would a city do that?
Chicago was built on swampy ground, barely above Lake Michigan. Water stood in the streets... and in eighteen fifty-four, cholera killed about one in twenty people.
The fix: lay sewers on top of the streets... bury them under feet of new dirt... and raise every building to the new level.
On Lake Street, six hundred men worked under a whole row of brick buildings. They set six thousand jackscrews... and turned them, a little at a time.
The block rose more than four feet. And business went on as usual the entire time.
Next, a six-story hotel went up six feet... with its guests still inside.
And that's how Chicago lifted an entire city block.
