## Topic research report (scriptwriting rules 2.G)

- Story: on 24 October 1901 Annie Edson Taylor, a widowed schoolteacher short of money who told people she was in her
  forties (records show she was in her sixties), became the first person to go over Niagara Falls in a barrel and
  survive. Her barrel was custom-built (oak and iron, padded); two days earlier a cat went over in it and came out
  alive. Helpers sealed her in and set the barrel adrift; it went over the Horseshoe Falls and she was pulled out
  about twenty minutes later with a cut on her head. Afterwards she warned others never to try it. Fame faded, her
  manager Frank Russell ran off with the barrel, and she died poor in 1921 (donations paid for her burial).
- Sources (agree on everything used): Wikipedia "Annie Edson Taylor"; HISTORY.com "First barrel ride over Niagara
  Falls" (This Day in History, Oct. 24); Mental Floss "Annie Taylor, the first person to cheat death over Niagara Falls".
- Left out or softened: her exact age (62 vs 63, sources differ) → "in her sixties"; "on her birthday" (Mental Floss
  says the day before) → not voiced; barrel material ("pickle barrel" vs oak and iron) → "a wooden barrel built,
  padded inside"; her exact quotes (only one source each) → "Her warning: nobody should ever try this again";
  whether the barrel was found again (sources differ) → "ran off with the barrel".
- Dangerous-acts check (compliance rulebook section 3): historical event, educational, her own warning voiced and
  labelled NEVER TRY THIS; no instructions; no injury shown beyond a small bandage.
- Demand: Niagara stunts are evergreen; "a teacher in her sixties" is a strong surprise hook.
