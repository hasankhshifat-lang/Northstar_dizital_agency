## Topic research report (scriptwriting rules 2.G)

- Story: Charles E. Boles robbed at least 28 Wells Fargo stagecoaches in Northern California (1875–1883), wearing a flour
  sack with eye holes and a bowler, holding a shotgun he never fired; he left two poems signed "Black Bart, the PO8".
  At his last robbery (3 November 1883, Funk Hill near Copperopolis) things went wrong and he fled, dropping a handkerchief
  with the laundry mark F.X.O.7. Detectives James Hume and Harry Morse checked about 90 San Francisco laundries until
  Ferguson & Bigg's California Laundry traced it to Boles, who lived as a respectable gentleman. Six years in San Quentin,
  released after four (January 1888), last seen February 1888.
- Sources (agree on everything used): Wikipedia "Black Bart (outlaw)"; Smithsonian "The Poetic Tale of Literary Outlaw
  Black Bart"; History.com This Day in History (Nov. 3); Legends of America (charges for the last robbery only, six years).
- Left out or softened: "afraid of horses / fled on foot" (one source); quoting the poems (not needed); whether he was
  hit by the rifle shot (sources differ) → "a robbery went wrong"; "never fired a shot" → "He never shot anyone".
- Demand: Wild West outlaws are a proven Shorts bucket; a gentleman bandit caught by a laundry mark is a clean

## Script (130 words, as voiced)

For eight years, a polite bandit robbed stagecoaches in California... and left poems behind.
So how did they catch him?
He stepped into the road with a shotgun and a flour sack over his head, took the Wells Fargo strongbox, and walked away. He never shot anyone. Twenty-eight robberies.
Twice, he left a poem, signed... Black Bart, the P O eight.
Then, in eighteen eighty-three, near Copperopolis, a robbery went wrong. He ran... and dropped a handkerchief.
On it was a tiny laundry mark. F, X, O, seven.
Detectives checked about ninety laundries in San Francisco... until one knew the mark.
It belonged to Charles Boles, a well-dressed gentleman.
He got six years in San Quentin. After four, he walked out... and vanished, the way he had for eight years.
