## Topic research report (scriptwriting rules 2.H)

1. **Proposed topic:** Barcelona's 6–1 win over PSG in the Champions League round of 16, second leg, Camp Nou, 8 March 2017.
   They had lost the first leg 4–0, and no team had ever come back from four goals down in a knockout tie.
2. **Why viewers care:** the biggest "impossible" comeback in football. It's famous worldwide, but most people only know
   the final score, not how close it came to failing.
3. **Central conflict:** after Cavani's goal (about 62', 3–1 on the night) Barcelona needed **three more goals**, because
   away goals counted double. With two minutes left they still needed three.
4. **Strongest angle:** "the 7 minutes that broke football": after Cavani's away goal the tie looked over, and the story
   is told minute by minute from the 88th, with a countdown clock on screen.
5. **Opening hook (first 5 s):** "Eighty-eight minutes gone. Barcelona need three goals. Nobody in history has ever done this."
6. **Three titles:**
   - The 7 Minutes That Broke Football
   - Barcelona Needed 3 Goals in 7 Minutes. This Is What Happened.
   - Why PSG Still Can't Forget March 8, 2017
7. **Expected interest:** very high (see the table). The topic has shown a large, steady audience over the last 12 months.
8. **Evergreen or trending:** evergreen. Coverage comes back every time Barcelona and PSG meet.
9. **Visual opportunities:** a giant countdown clock, an aggregate scoreboard that flips, a drawn pitch with arrows for
   each goal, the crowd's reaction, the PSG bench's faces at 4–0 → 5–5 → 6–5, the last cross drawn in slow motion.
10. **Sources to check while scripting:** UEFA's official match report and timeline, BBC Sport and The Guardian reports
    (8 March 2017), and FC Barcelona's and PSG's official sites. Rule: every minute, scorer and score must match the
    official UEFA timeline. Refereeing controversies (the penalty decisions) are presented as debated opinions, not as fact.
11. **Score: 92/100.** Curiosity 16/20 · emotion 13/15 · suspense 15/15 · demand 15/15 · unexpected turns 10/10 ·
    visuals 9/10 · discussion 9/10 · reliable sources 5/5.

## Script v1: long-form (~9 min, ~1,300 words of narration)

Narration is in English (VibeVoice, voice Frank). Numbers are written as words, and "..." marks pauses.
On-screen text is in [brackets]. Style: Comic History Cartoon, with players drawn as caricatures in club colours, no crests and no logos.

### ACT 1: THE SETUP (0:00–2:00)

**[0:00 HOOK]**
[A huge stadium clock: 88:00. The scoreboard reads "AGGREGATE 4–5". A drawn crowd frozen mid-scream.]

> Eighty-eight minutes gone. Barcelona need two more goals. Not one... two. And if they score just one, they're still out.
> In the entire history of the Champions League... no team had ever come back from where Barcelona started this night.

**[0:12]**
[Clock rewinds fast. Title card: THE 7 MINUTES THAT BROKE FOOTBALL]

> To understand how impossible this was... we have to go back three weeks. To Paris. To a night Barcelona wanted to forget.

**[0:25: First leg]**
[Paris at night, a drawn Parc des Princes in navy and red. Date tag: 14 FEB 2017]

> February fourteenth, twenty seventeen. Barcelona arrive with Messi, Suárez and Neymar... the most feared attack on the planet.
> Eighteen minutes in, Ángel Di María curls a free kick over the wall. One nil.

[Di María caricature kissing the ball; a party hat pops onto his head: BIRTHDAY]

> It's his birthday. And he's not done.
> Draxler makes it two. Di María scores again. Then Edinson Cavani smashes in a fourth... and Cavani? It's his birthday too.

[Scoreboard slams: PSG 4–0 BARCELONA. Barcelona players drawn in grey, heads down.]

> Four nil. Barcelona's own coach, Luis Enrique, admitted their chances were all but over.

**[1:15: Why it's impossible]**
[Chalkboard-style graphic: 0 TEAMS HAVE EVER DONE THIS]

> To go through, Barcelona now need to win by five clear goals. And no team in Champions League history had ever overturned a four-nil first leg.
> Fans joked about it. Newspapers wrote the obituary. The tie, everyone agreed... was finished.
> Everyone except one man.

### ACT 2: THE ESCALATION (2:00–5:00)

**[2:00: The promise]**
[Press room, flashbulbs. Luis Enrique caricature leaning into the microphones.]

> The day before the second leg, Luis Enrique sat in front of the press and said something that sounded crazy.
> As reported in the papers the next day... if PSG could score four against Barcelona, Barcelona could score six.
> Six. Against one of the best defences in Europe.
> It sounded like a coach trying to keep his team believing. Nobody knew it would turn out to be a prediction.

**[2:40: Camp Nou]**
[Wide shot: Camp Nou at night, 96,290 drawn fans waving blue-and-red scarves. Date tag: 8 MAR 2017]

> March eighth. Ninety-six thousand people pack the Camp Nou. And Barcelona need a goal early... to make PSG nervous.
> They get it in three minutes. Luis Suárez, head first, from almost on the goal line. One nil.

[Aggregate counter flips: 1–4]

**[3:20: Momentum]**
> PSG look shaken. Just before half-time, a cross flies in, and PSG defender Layvin Kurzawa, trying to clear, turns it into his own net. Two nil.
> Five minutes into the second half, Neymar goes down in the box. Penalty. Messi steps up... three nil.

[Aggregate counter: 3–4. The crowd rising. Drawn PSG bench: coach Unai Emery staring at the pitch.]

> One more goal and the tie is level. The Camp Nou is shaking. PSG are on the ropes.
> And then... everything falls apart.

**[4:10: The goal that ended it]**
[Music cuts. Silence. A slow, drawn shot of Cavani turning in the box.]

> Sixty-two minutes. Cavani hits a volley... and it's in. Three-one on the night.

[Big on-screen rule card: AWAY GOALS COUNT DOUBLE]

> And that one goal changes all the maths. Back then, away goals counted double in a tie.
> Barcelona now need three more goals... in under thirty minutes... without letting in another.
> The noise in the stadium drops. Some fans head for the exits.

### ACT 3: THE TURNING POINT (5:00–7:45)

**[5:00: The long wait]**
[Clock ticking: 70… 75… 80… 85. Messi, Suárez and Neymar drawn exhausted, hands on knees.]

> Seventy minutes. Eighty. Eighty-five. Barcelona attack and attack... and nothing goes in.
> Eighty-seven minutes on the clock. Three goals needed. The dream is over.
> But Neymar... hasn't given up.

**[5:40: 88']**
[Clock: 88:00. Free kick, about twenty-five metres out. Neymar standing over the ball.]

> Eighty-eight minutes. Free kick. Neymar... over the wall... into the top corner. Four-one.
> It's a beautiful goal. But most people still think it's too late.

[Countdown banner: NEED 2 · TIME LEFT: 2 MIN + STOPPAGE]

**[6:15: 90+1']**
> Stoppage time. Barcelona are so desperate, even their goalkeeper, ter Stegen, runs forward for a free kick.
> Moments later, Suárez goes down in the box under a challenge from Marquinhos... and the referee points to the spot.
> PSG fans still argue about that decision today.
> This time, Neymar takes it... and sends the keeper the wrong way. Five-one.

[Aggregate counter: 5–5. Text flashes: PSG STILL GO THROUGH ON AWAY GOALS]

> Five-five on aggregate. And PSG are still going through, on that one away goal.
> One more. Barcelona need one more. And there are about three minutes left.

**[7:00: 90+5']**
[Everything slows. The clock reads 94:XX. Barcelona win one last free kick. Every Barcelona player runs forward.]

> Final seconds. One last free kick. It's rolled short to Neymar... and he lifts the ball into the box.
> PSG's defenders back away. And the man who gets there... isn't Messi. It isn't Suárez.
> It's Sergi Roberto. A substitute who came on in the seventy-sixth minute... a Catalan kid from Barcelona's academy, La Masia... stretching out his foot.

[Freeze frame: ball in mid-air between his toe and the net. One full second of silence.]

> And it's in.

### ACT 4: THE PAYOFF (7:45–9:00)

**[7:45: The explosion]**
[The drawn stadium erupts. Seismograph needle scribbling. On-screen: SEISMOGRAPHS 500 M AWAY RECORDED A SMALL TREMOR]

> Six-one. Six-five on aggregate. Ninety-six thousand people lose their minds.
> Half a kilometre away, seismographs at a science institute recorded a small tremor... caused by the celebrations.
> Barcelona had scored three goals in about seven minutes. Exactly what Luis Enrique had promised. Six.

**[8:15: What it meant]**
[Split screen: Barcelona players piled on Sergi Roberto / PSG players flat on the grass.]

> It became the first time a team had ever come back from four-nil down in a Champions League knockout tie.
> In Spain, they gave it a simple name... La Remontada. The comeback.
> For PSG, that night became a scar the club carried for years. Every time they reached the knockouts after that, people asked one question... could it happen again?

**[8:45: Final thought + CTA]**
[Back to the stadium clock from the hook, now stopped at 95:00.]

> Eighty-eight minutes of doubt. Seven minutes of madness. And one lesson football never forgets... it isn't over until the clock stops.
> Was this the greatest comeback ever... or does Liverpool in Istanbul beat it? Tell me in the comments.
> And subscribe... because next time, we're telling that story too.

[End card: Next story: ISTANBUL 2005]
