## Topic research report (scriptwriting rules 2.G)

- Story: on 15 January 1919, just after noon, a 50-ft steel tank holding ~2.3 million gallons of molasses in Boston's
  North End burst. A wave of molasses ran down Commercial Street at ~35 mph, broke the steel supports of the elevated
  railway, killed 21 people and injured ~150 (horses died too). It was unusually warm (over 40 °F); the molasses then
  thickened in the cold, trapping victims and slowing rescuers. The tank had leaked from the start. The owner (United
  States Industrial Alcohol) blamed a bomb; after years of hearings a court-appointed auditor (Hugh W. Ogden) ruled the
  company responsible; it paid $628,000.
- Sources (agree on everything used): Wikipedia "Great Molasses Flood"; History.com "The Great Molasses Flood of 1919"
  (Evan Andrews); NPR (2019-01-13/15 centenary pieces) for date, deaths and the 2.3 million gallons.
- Left out because the sources disagree: wave height (15 ft History.com / 25 ft Wikipedia / 40 ft plaque) → "a brown wall
  of syrup", no number; "painted brown to hide leaks" (Wikipedia only) → just "it leaked so badly, molasses dripped into
  the street" (History.com: "often leaked molasses onto the street").
- Demand: disasters + "weird but true" travel well in the 430-Short research; the molasses flood is a known viral
  history topic (centenary coverage, many 1M+ Shorts on it), but not yet on this channel.

## Script (119 words, as voiced)

In nineteen nineteen, a wave of molasses tore through Boston... and killed twenty-one people.
How can syrup kill?
Boston's North End. A steel tank, fifty feet tall, held two point three million gallons of molasses. It leaked so badly, molasses dripped into the street.
January fifteenth was oddly warm. Just after noon... the tank burst open.
A brown wall of syrup rolled down Commercial Street at thirty-five miles an hour. It snapped the legs of the elevated railway. It swept up wagons, horses, and people.
Then the cold set in. The molasses thickened like glue... and held anyone it caught.
The company blamed a bomb. A court blamed the leaking tank that sent a wave of molasses through Boston.
