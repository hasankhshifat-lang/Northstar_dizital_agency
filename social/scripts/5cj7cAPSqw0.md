It's January 1925, Nome, Alaska. Children are choking to death. Dr. Curtis Welch knows the killer: diphtheria.
His antitoxin has expired. The nearest fresh supply is in Anchorage, more than 500 miles away. The harbor is
frozen. The planes can't fly in this cold. The only road left is the dog sled trail.
A train rushes the serum to Nenana. Then 20 mushers take it, one after another. "Wild Bill" Shannon goes first
at 50 below zero. His face turns black with frostbite, but he hands it on. Leonhard Seppala and his old lead
dog, Togo, run 170 miles out to meet the relay. Then they race back across the cracking sea ice of Norton Sound
in the dark with the serum on board.
The last leg belongs to Gunnar Kaasen and a lead dog named Balto. A blizzard hits with winds near 80 mph.
A gust flips the sled. The serum vanishes into the snow. Kaasen digs for it with his bare hands until he finds it.
The next musher is asleep, so he does not stop. At 5:30 in the morning, February 2nd, his sled slides into Nome.
5 and a half days. Every child got the serum. Whose run was harder, Balto's or Togo's?
