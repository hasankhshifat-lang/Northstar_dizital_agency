### Facts used (2+ sources agree)
- Lytle S. Adams, a Pennsylvania dentist/dental surgeon, sent the plan to the White House in January 1942, weeks after Pearl
  Harbor (Wikipedia, Army, Air & Space Forces); Roosevelt approved it (Wikipedia, Air & Space Forces quoting the CWS history).
- Bats from caves in the Southwest (Army: search across the Southwest; NPS: Carlsbad Caverns; Air & Space: Texas caves).
- Chilled into hibernation (NPS, Army, Air & Space); small napalm incendiary with a timer/delay fuse attached to each bat
  (Wikipedia napalm, Army 17.5 g napalm + timer, NPS fuse); dropped in a case/canister that opens in the air (Army, Air & Space,
  Wikipedia canister caption); bats roost in wooden buildings (NPS, Wikipedia canister page).
- 1943, Carlsbad Army Airfield (auxiliary field), New Mexico: armed bats got loose and set buildings on fire (Wikipedia, NPS,
  Army); a hangar and a general's car (Air & Space Forces, Defense Media Network).
- About $2 million; canceled by the Navy (Fleet Admiral King) because it would not be combat-ready until mid-1945 (Wikipedia,
  Air & Space Forces).
- Left out: number of bats per bomb (1,040 vs 180 per container: sources differ), exact fire date (15 May 1943 in Wikipedia; a
  March date elsewhere), "ten times more fires" (one source), the atomic-bomb link (speculative), FDR's "not a nut" note (one source).

## Script (150 words, VibeVoice: numbers as words, no tags)

In nineteen forty-three, the US Army strapped bombs to bats... and the bats set their own airbase on fire. How did it go so wrong?
The plan came from a dentist. Weeks after Pearl Harbor, Lytle Adams sent the White House an idea... and President Roosevelt approved it.
Catch bats from caves in the Southwest. Chill them until they fall asleep. Clip a tiny napalm bomb with a timer to each one.
Drop them over Japan, in a case that opens in the air. The bats wake up... crawl into wooden buildings... and the timers go off.
But at an airfield in New Mexico, armed bats got loose. They flew into the buildings... and set a hangar on fire, and a general's car.
After two million dollars, the Navy canceled it. The bats would not be ready until nineteen forty-five.
It all started when the Army strapped bombs to bats.
