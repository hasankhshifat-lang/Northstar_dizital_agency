## Script (VibeVoice, Frank; no audio tags, numbers as words)
1. Six people on a church picnic in Oregon... were killed by a bomb from Japan. No plane ever came near. So how did it get there?
2. Japan built more than nine thousand balloons... out of paper, glued together by schoolgirls.
3. Each one carried bombs, and rode the jet stream across the Pacific Ocean... in about three days.
4. Hundreds landed in North America, from Alaska to Mexico.
5. But America kept it secret. Newspapers printed nothing... so Japan would think the balloons had failed.
6. Then, on May fifth, nineteen forty-five, near Bly, Oregon, Elsie Mitchell and five children found one in the woods, while her husband parked the car.
7. Someone pulled at it... and it exploded.
8. They were the only people killed by enemy attack on the American mainland in World War Two.
9. And Japan had already given up... believing every balloon had failed. Weeks before six people went on a picnic. (loops to line 1)

Fact check (only what the sources agree on): about 9,300 balloons launched Nov 1944–Apr 1945; envelopes of washi paper glued with konnyaku paste, largely by schoolgirls; hydrogen-filled, carried incendiaries and an anti-personnel bomb on a ring with sandbag ballast; rode the jet stream ~3 days; roughly 300+ found/reported in North America, from Alaska to northern Mexico (and as far east as Michigan); the US Office of Censorship asked the press to stay silent so Japan would not learn of results; Japan ended launches in April 1945; 5 May 1945 near Bly (Gearhart Mountain area), Elsie Mitchell (pregnant) and five children (ages 11–14) found a balloon while Rev. Archie Mitchell parked the car; it exploded when disturbed, killing all six; the only deaths from enemy action on the US mainland in WWII. Left out: names/ages of the children on screen, Elsie's pregnancy, exact cause of the detonation (who touched it is not known: "someone pulled at it" is the reported "disturbed it"), the Mitchell Monument, the 1987 paper cranes.
