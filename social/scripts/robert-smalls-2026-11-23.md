### Facts used (2+ sources agree)
- The white officers went ashore for the night and left the ship to the enslaved crew (NPS, Wikipedia, Battlefield Trust).
- About 3 a.m. the crew fired the boilers; they stopped at another wharf for Smalls's wife and children and other families (NPS, Wikipedia).
- Smalls wore the captain's straw hat to look like the captain from a distance (Wikipedia, Battlefield Trust/PBS).
- He gave the correct whistle signals; Fort Sumter let them pass (Wikipedia, NPS "knew the proper signals", Battlefield Trust).
- A white bedsheet was raised; the USS *Onward* nearly fired until the sheet was seen; Smalls surrendered the ship (Wikipedia, Battlefield Trust).
- Elected to Congress (1874) and bought his former enslaver's house in Beaufort (NPS, Wikipedia).
- Left out: exact head count (NPS "sixteen enslaved people" vs Wikipedia's 8 crew + 9 family), exact time past Sumter
  (4:15 vs 4:30 a.m.), the codebook and prize money (single-source detail for this cut).
- "Warship": the *Planter* was an armed Confederate transport with its own guns and was carrying cannon to the forts
  (Wikipedia, NPS); the narration calls it "an armed steamer" when it is introduced.

## Script (150 words, VibeVoice: numbers as words, no tags)

In eighteen sixty-two, an enslaved man stole a Confederate warship... and sailed it past Fort Sumter. How did he get away with it?
Robert Smalls steered the Planter, an armed steamer in Charleston. One night in May, the white officers went ashore... and left the ship to the enslaved crew.
At three in the morning, Smalls fired the boilers. He stopped at another wharf... and picked up his wife and children.
He put on the captain's straw hat. From the fort, he looked like the captain.
At Fort Sumter, he gave the right whistle signal... and the fort let him pass.
A Union ship nearly fired... until its crew spotted a white bedsheet on the mast.
Smalls handed over the ship... and everyone aboard was free.
He was later elected to Congress... and bought the house where he was once enslaved.
It started the night he stole a Confederate warship.
