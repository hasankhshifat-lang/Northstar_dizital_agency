## Script (VibeVoice, Frank; no audio tags, numbers as words)
1. To drive to this part of Minnesota... you have to go through Canada. Why is a piece of America stuck up there?
2. In seventeen eighty-three, after the Revolution, Britain and America drew a new border... using the best map they had.
3. The treaty said the line would run to the northwest corner of Lake of the Woods... then west, to the Mississippi.
4. But the map was wrong. The lake was a maze of bays... and the Mississippi started far to the south.
5. In eighteen eighteen, they patched it. From that corner, the line dropped straight south... to the forty-ninth parallel.
6. That one line cut off a piece of America.
7. It's the only part of the lower forty-eight north of that line. To drive home, people cross about sixty miles of Canada... then check in with border officers on a screen, in a little shed.
8. All because of one bad map. (loops to line 1)

Fact check (only what the sources agree on): 1783 Treaty of Paris drew the line through Lake of the Woods to its "northwesternmost point", then "on a due west course to the river Mississippi"; negotiators used the 1755 Mitchell Map, which drew the lake as an oval and the Mississippi west of it; the Mississippi actually rises south of the lake (Lake Itasca), so a due-west line never meets it; the 1818 convention ran the line from that point due south to the 49th parallel, then west along it; the cut-off piece is the only part of the lower 48 north of the 49th parallel; by road it is reached through Manitoba, about 60 miles (Wikipedia ~63 mi from Warroad/Roseau; CBS ~60 mi); travelers report to US Customs by video at an unstaffed kiosk at Jim's Corner (CBS: video phone; Wikipedia: iPad kiosk). Left out: population (2020 census 149 vs CBS ~60 year-round: they measure different things), "14,000 islands" (not in the checked sources), the one-room school (cut for length), the 2021 ice road. The 1755 map is shown as "as the 1755 map showed it" (a simplified drawing, labelled); the exact corner was only fixed by the 1825 survey (not claimed in the script).
