## Script (VibeVoice, Frank; no audio tags, numbers as words)
1. She was on the Titanic when it sank... and on both of its sister ships when they were hit. How did one woman survive all three?
2. Violet Jessop was a ship's stewardess.
3. In nineteen eleven, she was aboard the Olympic when a British warship rammed it. It limped back to port.
4. Seven months later, she was on the Titanic. As it sank, she was ordered into a lifeboat... and, she later wrote, an officer handed her a baby.
5. On the rescue ship, a woman took the baby... without a word.
6. Then came World War One. Violet became a nurse on the third sister, the Britannic.
7. In nineteen sixteen, it hit a mine. Her lifeboat was pulled toward the spinning propellers. She jumped... was sucked under... and hit her head on the keel.
8. She survived that too. She went back to sea for decades... and died at eighty-three, on dry land. (loops to line 1)

Fact check (only what the sources agree on): stewardess on RMS Olympic during the HMS Hawke collision off the Isle of Wight, 20 Sep 1911, no deaths, Olympic returned to Southampton; stewardess on Titanic, ordered into a lifeboat to set an example; her memoir says an officer gave her a baby to mind and a woman (presumed the mother) took it from her on the Carpathia without a word; WWI nurse (VAD, British Red Cross) on HMHS Britannic, which hit a mine in the Kea Channel on 21 Nov 1916; her lifeboat was drawn toward the turning propellers, she jumped, was pulled under and struck her head on the keel (her own account); later served on liners (Red Star, Royal Mail Lines) until retiring in 1950; retired to Great Ashfield, Suffolk, kept hens; died 1971 aged 83. Left out: the lifeboat number (sources say 16 or 6: disputed), the toothbrush anecdote, "Miss Unsinkable" (a later nickname), the skull fracture diagnosis (from later headaches; secondary).
