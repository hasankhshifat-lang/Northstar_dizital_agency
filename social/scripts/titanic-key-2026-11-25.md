## Topic research report (scriptwriting rules 2.G)

- Story: at Southampton on 9 April 1912, the day before the maiden voyage, White Star moved Henry Wilde (from the
  Olympic) in as chief officer; Murdoch and Lightoller stepped down a rank and Second Officer David Blair was taken off
  the ship. He left with a key from the crow's nest. The lookouts had no binoculars on the voyage and had been told
  there were none for them. At 11:40 pm on 14 April Fred Fleet saw the iceberg with his naked eye, rang the bell and
  phoned the bridge. At the US Senate inquiry he was asked how much sooner binoculars would have shown it: "Enough to
  get out of the way." The key was sold at Henry Aldridge & Son on 22 September 2007 for £90,000.
- **Accuracy angle (the reason for the script's wording):** popular sources say the key opened the binocular locker;
  Wikipedia (David Blair) says the locker held the crow's-nest telephone and Blair's own binoculars were probably left
  in his cabin. So the script never claims the key locked up the binoculars: it asks the question and says
  "Historians still argue" (the queue's hook "The binoculars on the Titanic were locked away" was not used).
- Sources (agree on everything used): Wikipedia "David Blair (mariner)" (reshuffle, key, Fleet testimony, auction
  £90,000, telephone-locker caveat); The Irish Post "A last minute crew change and the key that could have saved the
  Titanic" (Blair replaced at the last minute, key in his pocket, Fleet's testimony, £90,000); The Courier (Dundee)
  "Key carried by Dundee man might have prevented Titanic sinking" (search summary).
- Left out: who bought it (sources differ: Chinese collector / TESIRO), the US$ price (one source), the postcard.
- Demand: Titanic is the channel's best-travelling bucket (ctx0tm_C56c 1,259 views, 79% viewed); "one small object
