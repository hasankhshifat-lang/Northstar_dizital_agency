# Spotlight Effect — 10-second style test (psychology channel)

Status: storyboard (pre-production). Format: YouTube Short / Facebook Reel, 9:16, 1080×1920, 10 s.
Purpose: the first test for the new psychology niche. One topic and one narration are told in three Lemo-Opuscar
styles (Marker on Paper, Whiteboard Explainer, Stick-figure Explainer). The only thing that changes is the style, so
the results are a fair comparison (animation-direction-rules §8.4: change one variable at a time).

## Topic research report (scriptwriting-rules §2.H)

1. **Topic:** the spotlight effect: people think others notice them far more than they actually do.
2. **Why viewers care:** almost everyone has felt "everyone saw that". The answer is relieving and easy to share.
3. **Central question:** how many people actually noticed?
4. **Angle:** a real experiment with a guessed number against the real number. The gap between them is the payoff.
5. **Hook (0–2 s):** "Everyone noticed that… right?" over the embarrassing moment.
6. **Titles:** "Nobody Noticed (Science Says So)" · "The Spotlight Effect in 10 Seconds" · "Why You Think Everyone Is Watching You"
7. **Audience interest:** high. This matches the "psychology of…" formats that small channels get millions of views
   with (YouTube Data API check, 2026-11-15: median 1.44M views for the top 25 psychology explainers of the last 12 months).
8. **Evergreen.**
9. **Visual opportunities:** one person in the shirt, a room of others, heads that turn or don't, a guessed count
   against a real count.
10. **Source:** Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000), "The spotlight effect in social judgment", *Journal of Personality
    and Social Psychology* 78(2). Cornell students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room of peers. The wearers
    guessed about half the room would notice; about a quarter actually did.
    **Before the final render, check the exact percentages against the paper.** The narration uses only the rounded
    "half" and "one in four".
11. **Score: 81/100.** Curiosity 16/20 · emotion 12/15 · suspense 9/15 · demand 13/15 · unexpected 8/10 ·
    visual 9/10 · comments 9/10 · research 5/5. Strong candidate.

## Narration (shared by all three versions, VibeVoice voice Frank)

> Everyone noticed that... right? Cornell students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room. They guessed half the room noticed. Real number? About one in four.

25 words, about 9.5 s at a natural pace. Numbers are written as words for VibeVoice. Beat map used by every storyboard:

|---|---|---|

## Storyboard A — Marker on Paper (`marker-paper`)

Rules of the style used here: one matte white sheet fills the frame. Nothing is drawn by a visible hand or pen; the
strokes appear on their own. Black builds everything, **blue = the given (the guess)** and **red = the wanted
(the real number)**. Red is the last stroke on a sheet and is held at least 1.5 s. The camera is top-down and only
moves over the paper. A new question means a peel.

| # | Time | Narration | Purpose | Shot + camera (why) | Drawing: position, action, "eyes", expression | Contact / ink logic | Paper / environment | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 0.0–2.0 | "Everyone noticed that... right?" | Hook: surprising moment | Close on a small stick person in the upper third, then **pull back** at 1.2 s to reveal 8 small eyes (circle + dot) drawn around them (to show how big the worry feels) | Person drawn first (head, body, slumped shoulders = embarrassed). A black shirt outline with one red question mark on it. The eyes pop on one by one, all pupils pointing at the person | The eyes are separate strokes; the pupil dot lands after the circle. Red "?" is the last stroke | Question written top-left in Kalam Bold: "Who noticed?"; page number ① circled top-right | Marker squeak per stroke; a small "tick" as each eye lands |
| A2 | 2.0–5.2 | "Cornell students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room." | New information: the experiment | **Peel** to sheet ② (a new step). Locked top-down, then **follow the pen** along a door-to-room arrow (to follow the student's path) | A doorway (two lines) on the left; the student figure is redrawn walking in, 3 steps drawn as 3 poses on twos. A room outline holds a 4×3 grid of tiny seated heads (12 people) | Feet touch the floor line in each pose (no floating). The arrow is drawn as the shaft first, then the two head strokes | Label "CORNELL, 2000" in small black notes | Paper peel rustle; soft footstep tick on each drawn step |
| A3 | 5.2–7.4 | "They guessed half the room noticed." | The given (the guess) | **Lean in** (1.4×) on the grid (this is the number we test) | A thought bubble from the student. **Blue** circles go around 6 of the 12 heads, left to right. Blue "½" written in the bubble | Each blue circle overlaps past closure and darkens where it crosses black | — | One soft blue-marker squeak per circle (6, fast) |
| A4 | 7.4–8.4 | "Real number?" | Turning point | **Pull back** to the full grid (the comparison has to be read at once) | A black line crosses out the blue "½" (the guess is challenged). A red "?" appears next to it | The cross-out stroke runs through the existing blue ink and darkens where it crosses | — | Short marker scratch |
| A5 | 8.4–10.0 | "About one in four." | Payoff | Hold, then a slow **lean in** to the red answer (the answer matters most) | **Red** circles land on only 3 of the 12 heads; the other 9 stay black. Red "¼" written large (110 px) under the grid. Red is the last stroke and is held 1.6 s | Red circles sit exactly on 3 of the blue-circled heads (the noticers were among the guessed) | Board stays still except for the line boil | Reveal hit on the "¼" stroke |

Checks: the hook visual is on screen by 0.4 s · every sheet has one red focus · no character stands and talks ·
the camera moves only for a stated reason · all content stays above y = 1520.

## Storyboard B — Whiteboard Explainer (`whiteboard`)

Rules of the style used here: one glossy dry-erase board, 8000 × 4500 board space. Ghost marks of old lessons show at
5–10 %. Floating markers cast shadows (no hand). Magnets and an eraser move on their own. The camera travels between
board regions instead of cutting. Colours: black `#23262c` = people and structure; blue `#2a5cb3` = the guess;
orange `#d97757` = the one thing that matters (the real number).

| # | Time | Narration | Purpose | Shot + camera (why) | Drawing / props: position, action, expression | Contact / object logic | Board / environment | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 0.0–2.0 | "Everyone noticed that... right?" | Hook | Tight on region 1 (one figure), then **pull back from a tiny drawing** (to show a small moment feeling huge) | The black marker flies in, its shadow shrinks as it touches down, and it draws a figure with hunched shoulders and a hand over the face (embarrassed). An orange "!" on the shirt. Eight black arrows are drawn from the edges toward the figure | The marker nib stays on the board for every stroke and lifts between strokes (the shadow grows). It parks off-frame after the last arrow | The window sheen drifts across at 0.35× the camera speed | Marker squeak per stroke; nib-tap on touch-down |
| B2 | 2.0–5.2 | "Cornell students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room." | New information | **Ride the line** the pen draws: a path from region 1 to region 2 (the student walks into the room) | The marker draws a dashed path, then a room rectangle (4 strokes, corners overshoot). **Twelve round magnets** drop into a 4×3 grid one row at a time, each with a shrinking shadow (these are the people in the room). The student figure is a hero magnet that slides along the path into the doorway | Each magnet drops, its shadow shrinks, and it lands with a click; the hero magnet slides with a small lift and settles at the door | "CORNELL · 2000" written in the room corner as it is said | Magnet clicks on landing (3 rows = 3 clusters); a slide whoosh for the hero |
| B3 | 5.2–7.4 | "They guessed half the room noticed." | The given | **Hold still** on the grid (read it now) | The blue marker flies in and circles 6 magnets; a speech bubble from the hero magnet: "½?" in blue | The circles start at an angle and overlap past closure; the ink darkens over black | — | 6 quick squeaks |
| B4 | 7.4–8.4 | "Real number?" | Turning point | **Whip** to the grid's right edge where the eraser waits (a "but") | The felt eraser slides on its own across the 6 blue circles and wipes them to a ghost (the guess is undone, but the ghost stays readable) | The eraser moves at constant speed along a path, about 90 % strength, so a ghost remains | — | A felt-wipe swish |
| B5 | 8.4–10.0 | "About one in four." | Payoff | **Hold**, framed so the grid and the answer read at once; a tiny dolly-in at 9.2 s | The orange marker circles only 3 magnets; the other 9 magnets turn slightly away (a small rotation, so the people who didn't notice look away). Orange "¼" written large beside the grid on the word "four" | The orange circles land on 3 magnets that keep their shadows; the turned magnets rotate about their centres without sliding | Ghost of the erased blue still visible: the guess and the reality on one board | Reveal hit as the "¼" finishes |

Checks: writing finishes on the spoken word · one accent per region · the camera travels, never jump-cuts ·
props never float without a shadow · subtitles in the off-white label just above y = 1520.

## Storyboard C — Stick-figure Explainer (`stick-explainer`)

Rules of the style used here: warm paper `#F3F0EA`, ink figures with one even line weight, **no faces and no hands**,
one accent `#EB4A2A`. Emotion is shown through posture and head turns, since there are no faces. Figures spring
between poses (forearms arrive late) and grow out of the ground line. Type is Jost, left-aligned on a 124 px margin.
The accent means only one thing: **the people who really noticed**. The worried student is drawn in ink with an
accent shirt mark only at the payoff, when it becomes "the thing that matters".

| # | Time | Narration | Purpose | Shot + camera (why) | Figures: position, action, head direction, posture | Contact / object logic | Environment | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | 0.0–2.0 | "Everyone noticed that... right?" | Hook | **Locked frame**, medium: one figure centre, on the ground line (to set up the rule: one person, one worry) | The student pops out of the ground with a spring (about 20 % overshoot), then shrinks: shoulders drop, head tilts down, arms pull close to the body (a short anticipation dip first). A thin shirt outline on the torso. Bold title, left: "Everyone saw that?" | Feet stay planted on the ground line during the shrink (no sliding) | Paper and margins never move | Soft "pop" on spawn; a low "hmm" note under the shrink |
| C2 | 2.0–5.2 | "Cornell students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room." | New information | **Pull back** (one person becomes a room) | The student walks in from the left with alternating legs and settles. Twelve figures grow out of three ground lines (staggered rows, no two the same timing): some sit, some stand, all with their own breathing and sway. Small caps label: "CORNELL STUDY · 2000" | Feet keep contact on each step, with weight shift; the walk decelerates and settles into a balanced pose | Three light-grey ground lines (the room) | Light footsteps on foot contact; a soft room murmur |
| C3 | 5.2–7.4 | "They guessed half the room noticed." | The given | **Push in + focus shift** on the student (to show the guess comes from inside their head) | The student glances left and right (head turns, body follows late). A thought panel with "GUESS: 6 / 12" in big numbers. In the crowd, 6 figures are drawn with a light-grey head outline (the imagined watchers, shown in their imagined state) | The student's head turns before the shoulders (overlapping action) | The pale tint fills the thought panel only | A ticking count as 6 heads grey in |
| C4 | 7.4–8.4 | "Real number?" | Turning point | **Pull back** to the full room (the reality check needs the whole room) | The grey outlines fade (the imagined watchers disappear). All 12 figures carry on with their own business: two talk to each other (heads turned toward one another), one looks at the ground, and so on. Nobody moves in unison | Talking figures face each other; one leans in, the other answers with a small head nod on a beat (one nod only, motivated) | — | The murmur rises slightly |
| C5 | 8.4–10.0 (verdict frame 7.5–10.0) | "About one in four." | Payoff | **Locked frame** (verdict, reading time ≥ 2.5 s) | Exactly 3 figures turn their heads toward the student, each with a short delay, and their lines change to the accent colour. The student's shirt mark also turns accent. Big number left: "3 / 12", then the line beneath: "about 1 in 4 noticed". The student straightens up slightly (relief) | The head turns go through springs; the bodies follow late. The 3 are spread through the room so they read on a phone | — | Three soft ticks as the heads turn; the reveal hit on "four" |

Checks: no faces needed, since posture carries the emotion · characters react to each other in C4–C5 ·
the accent has one meaning only · verdict frame held ≥ 2.5 s · subtitle line at bottom-left just above y = 1520.
