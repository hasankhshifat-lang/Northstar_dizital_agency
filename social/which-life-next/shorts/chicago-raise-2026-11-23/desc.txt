Chicago was built on swampy ground barely above Lake Michigan; water stood in the streets, and cholera killed about one in twenty people in 1854. From 1856 the city laid sewers on top of the streets, buried them under new dirt, and raised the buildings to the new level. On Lake Street in 1860, 600 men used 6,000 jackscrews to lift a whole row of brick buildings more than four feet while business went on, and in 1861 the six-story Tremont House hotel was raised about six feet with its guests inside.
