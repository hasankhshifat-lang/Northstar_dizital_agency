On January 15, 1919, a 50-foot steel tank holding 2.3 million gallons of molasses burst in Boston's North End. A wave of molasses rolled down Commercial Street at about 35 mph, snapped the supports of the elevated railway and killed 21 people; as the winter cold set in, the molasses thickened and trapped victims and rescuers. The company blamed a bomb, but a court-appointed auditor found the company and its leaking tank responsible.
