From November 1944 to April 1945, Japan launched more than 9,000 hydrogen balloons made of paper, many glued together by schoolgirls. Each carried bombs on the jet stream across the Pacific, and hundreds reached North America, from Alaska to Mexico. The US asked newspapers to stay silent so Japan would think the balloons had failed. On May 5, 1945, near Bly, Oregon, Elsie Mitchell and five children on a church picnic found one in the woods; it exploded, killing all six, the only deaths from enemy attack on the US mainland in World War II.
