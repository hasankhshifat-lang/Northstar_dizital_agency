The marathon at the 1904 St. Louis Olympics (August 30, 1904) started in about 90-degree heat on dusty roads with one water stop at mile 12. Fred Lorz rode part of the way in a car and was hailed as the winner until spectators exposed him; Thomas Hicks, fading, was given strychnine (a rat poison) mixed with egg whites, and later brandy, by his trainers, and won gold. A Cuban runner, Andarín Carvajal, ate rotten apples and took a nap; a wild dog chased Len Tau about a mile off course.
