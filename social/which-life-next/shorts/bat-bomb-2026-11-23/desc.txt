In 1942, weeks after Pearl Harbor, a Pennsylvania dentist named Lytle Adams sent the White House a plan: chill bats from Southwestern caves into hibernation, clip a tiny timed napalm bomb to each one, and drop them over Japan, where they would roost in wooden buildings. President Roosevelt approved it. In 1943, armed bats got loose at the Carlsbad Army Airfield in New Mexico and set a hangar and a general's car on fire. After about two million dollars, the Navy canceled the project (Project X-Ray) because it would not be ready until mid-1945.
