In nineteen forty-three, the US Army strapped bombs to bats... and the bats set their own airbase on fire. How did it go so wrong?
The plan came from a dentist. Weeks after Pearl Harbor, Lytle Adams sent the White House an idea... and President Roosevelt approved it.
Catch bats from caves in the Southwest. Chill them until they fall asleep. Clip a tiny napalm bomb with a timer to each one.
Drop them over Japan, in a case that opens in the air. The bats wake up... crawl into wooden buildings... and the timers go off.
But at an airfield in New Mexico, armed bats got loose. They flew into the buildings... and set a hangar on fire, and a general's car.
After two million dollars, the Navy canceled it. The bats would not be ready until nineteen forty-five.
It all started when the Army strapped bombs to bats.
