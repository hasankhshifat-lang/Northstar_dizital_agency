## Why the views are low

1. **The long videos get no traffic at all.** 4 views between them. Shorts views do not count
   toward the 4,000 hours, so right now the channel makes no progress on watch time.
2. **The topics are too narrow to be searched or suggested.** "First winter as a homesteader 1888"
   and "locust plague 1874" have little search demand. The format that is breaking out in this
   niche is **"How Long Would You Survive in Every ___"**:
   - Fossil: "How Long You'd Last in Every Prehistoric Era" — 12.2M views (50K-sub channel).
   - ONE RULE: "How Long Would You Survive in Every WW2 Job?" — 112.9K views from a 917-sub channel.
   - Santi Can't: "How Long You'd Last in Every Century…" — 255K views (8.9K subs).
   - "Living in Historical Eras Tier List" — 78K views (4.5K subs).
   - Search: "how long would you survive" ≈ 50K searches/month; "…every century of rome" competition
     12/100; "…a day in ancient rome" 15; "…american soldier in vietnam" 15; "…every ww2 job" 23;
     "why you would not survive a day in victorian london" +331% growth.
   One event per video gives one hook; "every era / every job" gives a new hook every 60–90 s,
   which is what keeps retention up on the winners.
3. **The long videos are short for watch-hour goals.** The winners run 13–16 min. Ours are 3–9 min.
4. **Shorts don't send viewers to the long videos.** The description link barely gets clicked. YouTube's
   "Related video" field on each Short (set in YouTube Studio) is the real Short → long link, and it
   is not set.
5. **Shorts don't turn into subscribers.** ~3,200 views → 2 subscribers. Shorts are one-offs with no
   series name, so viewers have no reason to follow.
6. **No data on clicks or retention.** Without the channel connected to vidIQ (or YouTube Studio
   numbers), we can't tell whether the thumbnail or the first 30 seconds is the problem.

## The honest math for 30 November

- 4,000 hours = 240,000 minutes. A 14-min video where viewers watch ~35% gives ~5 min per view, so
  we'd need **~49,000 long-form views in 30 days**. Today we have 4.
- 1,000 subscribers usually needs ~100K–300K views, mostly on long-form.
- That only happens if **at least one long video breaks out**. Daily posting alone won't get there.
  The plan below is built to give us the best chance, but hitting both numbers by 30 Nov is unlikely.
- Fallback goals: the lower YouTube Partner tier (500 subs + 3,000 hours, or 500 subs + 3M Shorts
  views in 90 days) unlocks memberships and Super Thanks; the full tier also accepts
  10M Shorts views in 90 days.

## What changes

1. **New long-form format: "How Long Would You Survive in Every ___" (American history), 12–16 min.**
   Kit lives through each era/job; a survival "timer" on screen; each section ends with the cause
   of death and a verdict. Same voice (Jesse), same character (Kit), same POV identity.
2. **Shorts: one section per Short, each ending on Kit's survival time** ("Survival time: 4 hours").
   Series name in every title: "How long would you last? #N". Shorts at 25–45 s (the cholera one,
   40 s, did best).
3. **Set the "Related video" on every Short** to that day's long video (you, in YouTube Studio —
   the API can't set it). Pin a comment with the link too.
4. **Thumbnails:** Kit's face + big survival timer ("3 MIN") + era label. Test 2 thumbnails per long
   video with YouTube Studio's "Test & compare".
5. **Connect the channel in vidIQ** so I can read CTR, retention and traffic sources in the daily
   report.
6. Keep 1 long + 3 Shorts a day only if quality holds; a 14-min "every era" video is ~2× the work.
   If it slips, go to 4–5 long videos a week and keep 3 Shorts every day.
