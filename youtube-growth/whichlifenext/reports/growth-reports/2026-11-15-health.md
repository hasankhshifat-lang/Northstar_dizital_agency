## 2. Strongest growth opportunities
1. **Packaging and retention already work on the best Shorts.** Avg % viewed 67–86% on the top five (Blizzard
   85.6%, Titanic baker 79.0%, Pony Express 69.8%). The topics that travelled were survival/disaster stories with a
   person at the centre.
2. **US reach was there and then disappeared**, which points to something we changed (see §3). The fix is a cheap,
   controlled test.
3. Search is small but real (263 views; "oregon trail", "abraham lincoln assassination", "john wilkes booth"). An
   evergreen long-form on those can collect search traffic over time.

## 3. Biggest weaknesses
1. **US share collapsed.** The 8 Shorts published 17:00–23:00 ET (10-28…11-04) got 6–49% of their views from the US
   (Blizzard 44%, Shortcut 47%, Mushers 49%). The 3 published at about 08:00 ET with real volume got 2%
   (Lincoln manhunt 2.1%, Titanic baker 2.4%).
   **Hypothesis, not proven.** The time slot changed (to 08:00 ET = 21:00 Singapore) together with the format
   (POV → story), the cadence (several a day → one a day) and the topics. The first test audience at 08:00 ET is
   mostly UK/Europe/Asia.
2. **Volume dropped after the burst.** Shorts from 11-10, 11-11 and 11-12 got 61–168 views with 2–3 days of data,
   and 11-10 had a 66% swipe-away (engaged 21/61).
3. **Long-form doesn't work yet** (3 videos, 3–10 views, 3–35% viewed). Don't make more long-form until a Shorts
   audience exists or there is a search-led plan.
4. **Subscriber conversion is low:** 17 subscribers from about 14k views (0.12%). There is no series promise or
   reason to return.

## 4. Content opportunities
- Person-in-peril survival stories (blizzard, Donner shortcut, Titanic baker) outperform objects and odd facts so
  far. EXP-firstframe-person-vs-object is already testing this; let it finish (≥3 per arm).
- A recurring series name for the survival stories, to give a reason to subscribe.
