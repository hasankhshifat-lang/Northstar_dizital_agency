### Diagnostic reports (Shorts below this week's 72 h median, growth-priority order)

Script lines are not on `main` for these Shorts (no `social/scripts/<ID>.md`), so drops are mapped to the hook line and
the beat after it from the learning database; exact line mapping = data gap.

**vy-qRhRjnN0 Ghost Army (61 views, engaged 34%, avg 49% viewed)**
1. What went wrong: two of three viewers swiped away at the first frame. 2. Metric: engaged ÷ views 0.34 (channel
week 0.55). 3. When: first second, then −14% at 5.2 s and −14% at 7.0 s (retention curve). 4. Likely cause: the hook
(priority 3), not demand: the Ghost Army is a known, searched story. The first frame was an object (a rubber tank in a
field) and the line "These tanks are made of rubber." gives no person or stakes; the 5–7 s drop sits on the beat after
the hook. 5. Evidence: lowest engaged ratio on the channel; the two person-first Shorts that week had 44% and 53%
swipe-away. 6. Change: open on a person and the stakes (a soldier inflating a tank while German recon flies over).
7. Test: already covered by EXP-firstframe-person-vs-object; don't add a new test.

**PKdcctcYjfw Headless chicken (170 views, engaged 56%, avg 40% viewed)**
1. Viewers stayed past the first frame but left early. 2. avg % viewed 40% (week 73%). 3. Watch ratio 0.90 at 6 s →
0.56 at 12 s → 0.40 at 17 s; drops at 6.2–12.9 s. 4. Likely cause: story (priority 4): the hook gives the whole payoff
("It kept walking") and the next beat explains instead of opening a new question. Topic demand is also lower for "weird
but true" (priority 1; hypothesis). 5. Evidence: normal first-second hold, steep mid-video loss; FB shows the same 3–4 s
dip. 6. Change: hold back part of the payoff and add a second question by 5 s ("…and then a farmer took it on tour").
7. Test: keep in the learning notes; no separate experiment while two run.

**_w_jqoz9elc Hugh Glass (81 views, engaged 47%, avg 75.5% viewed)**
1. Few viewers, but those who stayed watched three quarters. 2. Views stalled at 81 by ~30 h; engaged 0.47.
3. Drops at 1.2–1.8 s (first second), later losses small. 4. Likely cause: first frame (priority 3): a man lying still on
the ground, a static image that doesn't stop the scroll, although the topic has demand (The Revenant). 5. Evidence:
first-second drop dominates; good hold after it. 6. Change: start on motion (the bear charging, or Glass starting the
crawl). 7. Test: counts as a person-first data point in the first-frame experiment; note "static frame" as a variable
to separate later.
