## Script

### HOOK (0:00-0:11)

[ON SCREEN: Black. A tin cup fills with brown river water. POV hands lift it toward camera.
Day counter in the corner: DAY 1.]

What kills you first on the Oregon Trail? Not snakes, not bandits, but something you'll drink
before breakfast.

[ON SCREEN: Smash cut - title card "30 DAYS" stamped over a wagon wheel.]

### THE TURN (0:11-0:36)

[ON SCREEN: Independence, Missouri. Muddy street, crowded with wagons. POV walking through it.]

It's spring, 1850. You're in Independence, Missouri, and you're about to walk two thousand miles
to Oregon. You're not a real person - you're built from the diaries, guidebooks and records of
the people who actually did this. Everything that happens to you happened to them.

You get thirty days. Let's see if you make it.

### BEAT 1 - THE OUTFIT (0:36-1:19)

[ON SCREEN: A general store counter. Prices chalked on a board. POV hands counting coins.]

First, you shop. A guidebook from this year has a list for a family of three. Four yoke of oxen
- that's eight animals - at fifty dollars a yoke. Two hundred dollars. A wagon and its canvas
cover, another hundred.

[ON SCREEN: Flour sacks stacking up in the wagon bed, a counter ticking: 200 LB... 400... 600.]

Then food. The rule people repeat in letters home is two hundred pounds of flour per person. For
three of you, that's six hundred pounds of flour before you've bought a single slab of bacon.

[ON SCREEN: The loaded wagon. POV looks for a seat - there isn't one.]

Here's the first surprise. You don't ride in the wagon. It's full. You walk beside it. Every mile.

### BEAT 2 - THE CLOCK (1:19-1:58)

[ON SCREEN: A map of the trail unrolls. A small wagon icon at Missouri. A far-off rock marked
"JULY 4".]

You leave in spring for a reason. Leave too early and there's no grass for the oxen. Leave too
late and the snow catches you in the mountains.

[ON SCREEN: DAY 3. Dawn. POV yoking oxen, breath visible in the cold.]

So the whole trail runs on one deadline. There's a rock in what will become Wyoming - Independence
Rock. If you reach it by the Fourth of July, you're on schedule. If you don't, you're racing the
winter.

[ON SCREEN: The wagon icon creeps along the map. Odometer: 10... 15 miles.]

Most days you'll make ten to fifteen miles. On foot. At the pace of an ox.

### BEAT 3 - THE FIRST RIVER (1:58-2:29)

[ON SCREEN: DAY 6. A wide brown river. Wagons lined up at a ferry. POV looks down at the current.]

Then the rivers start. Some have ferries. Some you just have to cross.

[ON SCREEN: A wagon ahead tips on the ferry. Shouting. POV grips the side rail.]

Out here, accidents are the second biggest killer. Wheels roll over people. Guns go off by
mistake. And rivers drown people - in front of their families, a few yards from the bank.

[ON SCREEN: Your wagon makes it across. Wet oxen climbing the far bank.]

You get across. Plenty of people don't. And this still isn't the thing that kills you first.

### BEAT 4 - THE ROUTINE (2:29-3:05)

[ON SCREEN: DAY 12. Montage - wake in the dark, coffee over buffalo chips, walking, dust, walking,
circling the wagons at night.]

By day twelve, every day is the same day. Up before light. Yoke the oxen. Walk. Stop at noon.
Walk. Circle the wagons. Cook. Sleep on the ground.

[ON SCREEN: POV's boots, worn through at the toe.]

Your boots are already going. Your feet are worse. And the wagon in front of you has started leaving things behind - a dresser, a stove - because the oxen can't pull it all.

[ON SCREEN: A line of abandoned furniture beside the trail, stretching into the distance.]

You'll see a lot of that. People's whole lives, left in the grass.

### BEAT 5 - WHAT NOBODY HERE KNOWS YET (3:05-3:59)

[ON SCREEN: DAY 19. Morning. The man from the next wagon looks pale, sitting on a crate.]

Day nineteen. The man in the next wagon says he feels a little feverish. He's fine. He's eating.

[ON SCREEN: NOON. He's lying down, not moving. His wife is shouting for help.]

By noon, he can't stand up.

[ON SCREEN: EVENING. A shovel. A small mound beside the trail. Someone scratching a name into a
board.]

By evening, you're helping dig his grave.

[ON SCREEN: Slow push in on the tin cup from the opening.]

That's cholera. The Park Service says victims often died within twelve hours of the first
symptoms. Healthy at breakfast. Gone by supper.

Here's the thing nobody on this trail knows yet. Cholera lives in dirty water. And thousands of
people and animals are using the same rivers, the same campsites, the same water holes, one after
another, all summer.

[ON SCREEN: The cup, the river, the crowded campsite - all the same brown.]

That cup you drank from on day one? That's your answer. Not snakes. Not bandits. The
water.

### BEAT 6 - THE NUMBERS (3:59-4:24)

[ON SCREEN: A long view of the trail. Small wooden grave markers appear one by one along it.]

Disease killed more people on this trail than anything else - cholera most of all. Historians
argue about the total. The National Park Service estimates that nearly one in ten people who set
out never made it.

[ON SCREEN: Markers multiply until the trail is lined with them.]

The Park Service's own math puts it at ten to fifteen deaths for every mile of trail.

### PAYOFF - DAY 30 (4:24-4:46)

[ON SCREEN: DAY 30. Sunset over the Platte River. Your wagon, your oxen, your family - all still
there.]

Day thirty. You're alive. You've walked maybe three or four hundred miles.

[ON SCREEN: The map. The wagon icon has barely moved. The July 4 rock is still far away.]

That means you have more than fifteen hundred to go. Independence Rock is still weeks ahead. The
mountains are after that. And you'll be drinking from that river every single day.

### CLOSE (4:46-4:55)

[ON SCREEN: The POV view lowers to the cup in your hands. Freeze. Text: "WHICH LIFE NEXT?"]

You made it thirty days. Which life do you want to live next? Tell me in the comments.

## Why this hook

Hook A uses **The Question** formula: it asks the one thing every viewer who played the game
thinks they know the answer to ("dysentery", "snakes"), then refuses to answer until Beat 5 - so
the question stays open across the slowest stretch of the video.
